## Context

Plan 7.5 asks for PDF anchors made of a physical page index, the printed label when present, a text selection mapping and normalized rectangles with rotation metadata, with text offsets in Unicode scalars and native UTF-16 converted at the adapter boundary. Plan 9.2 asks for PDFKit display and selection, highlights stored in Sylvia rather than written into the PDF, overlays drawn from saved records, per-page extraction status with "No selectable text on this page" and no OCR, and a specific error for encrypted files. Plan 18.1 forbids launch actions and unsolicited URLs. Gate G0.1 asks the spike to prove multiline and rotation anchors. The evidence is in `Docs/Stage0/G01-PDF.md`.

## Decision

**Framework.** PDFKit (system framework) for display, selection, navigation and text extraction. No third-party PDF code. The adapter is `Spikes/G01-PDF/Sources/PDFAnchorKit` and is the only place PDFKit types and UTF-16 ranges appear.

**Opening a file.** Sylvia opens a stored copy read-only and never writes to it. In order:

1. Stream the file through SHA-256 (1 MiB chunks). Failure to read: `fileUnreadable`.
2. Structure check: `%PDF-` within the first 1,024 bytes, otherwise `notAPDF`; `%%EOF` within the last 1,024 bytes, otherwise `truncated`.
3. `PDFDocument(url:)`; `nil` is `unparseable`.
4. `isLocked` (a user password is needed) is `passwordProtected`. Sylvia never calls `unlock(withPassword:)`, never asks for a password and never tries one.
5. Zero pages is `noPages`.

Each error maps to its own message. A file that is encrypted but opens without a password (owner password only) opens for reading; if it does not allow copying, Sylvia offers no text features on it (status `restrictedByPermissions`), because PDFKit itself does not enforce that flag (see Evidence). This last rule was a proposal that needed the product owner's decision; the product owner approved it on 22 October 2026 (PO-05 (a): read only, with the reason shown, and no quotation, search or AI text), and confirmed that password-protected PDFs stay excluded (PO-06 (a)).

**Extraction.** Page text is `PDFPage.string`, kept exactly as returned (no normalization), with a SHA-256 of its UTF-8 bytes. Each page has a status: `text(scalarCount)`, `noSelectableText` (nil or whitespace-only text) or `restrictedByPermissions`. A page without text shows "No selectable text on this page"; it is never OCRed, never given guessed text, never searched and contributes nothing to an AI context pack. The extraction version is `{extractor: "pdfkit.PDFPage.string", adapter_version: 1, platform: "<OS> <version> (<build>)"}`, because PDFKit's extractor ships with the OS and can change with it. Only the CropBox's text is extracted (PDFKit's behaviour).

**Anchor (portable envelope, one `anchors[]` entry of the capture envelope, plan A.1).**

Real example: the `multiline` sample from `Docs/Stage0/evidence/G01-PDF/anchor-samples.json` (a point-to-point drag on physical page 3, printed label "1"; fixture asset ID).

```json
{
  "version": 1,
  "kind": "pdf",
  "asset_id": "00000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000001",
  "asset_sha256": "6ffa266bf32eea6c9bc2984d351bc55d19bd948c65714a4e6d2b9ab41d8138d1",
  "exact_text": "ts own counsel. Under a skin of grey ice the water\nstill moves, slowly, turning over the cold it gathered in the night. A walker\nwho stops at the",
  "extraction": {"extractor": "pdfkit.PDFPage.string", "adapter_version": 1, "platform": "iOS 26.5 (23F77)"},
  "segments": [{
    "page_index": 3,
    "page_label": "1",
    "page_rotation": 0,
    "page_box": {"name": "crop", "rect": [0, 0, 612, 792]},
    "text": "ts own counsel. Under a skin of grey ice the water\nstill moves, slowly, turning over the cold it gathered in the night. A walker\nwho stops at the",
    "text_range": {"unit": "unicode_scalar", "start": 48, "end": 193},
    "prefix": "age 4\nThe pond in winter keeps i",
    "suffix": " shore hears it settle.\nSnow lie",
    "line_rects": [[0.327438, 0.880644, 0.396593, 0.013889],
                   [0.117647, 0.861705, 0.577392, 0.013889],
                   [0.117647, 0.842765, 0.130886, 0.013889]],
    "page_text_sha256": "a4746ee93c002a1122b57c8fb100a7a44d2d5700b22cd541f84ad01634e6d570"
  }],
  "reader_locator": {"adapter": "pdfkit", "utf16_ranges": [{"page_index": 3, "location": 48, "length": 145}]}
}
```

- One segment per contiguous text range per page, in document order. A selection across pages has one segment per page; a selection from one column into the next is one segment when the page text runs column by column (it does in PDFKit for the fixture); a selection composed of separate ranges (`PDFSelection.add`) has one segment per range (PDFKit merges ranges that touch or overlap and orders them by page and position, whatever order they were added in).
- `page_label` is the printed label from the file's `/PageLabels`, or `null`. PDFKit returns "1", "2", ... for files without `/PageLabels`; Sylvia does not store those invented labels.
- Text offsets are Unicode scalar offsets into the stored page text. PDFKit's UTF-16 character indices are converted at the adapter boundary, exactly, and rejected (not rounded) if a boundary falls inside a surrogate pair. They are kept only in the adapter-opaque `reader_locator`.
- A selection that ends with a line's last character: PDFKit reports its range (`PDFSelection.range(at:on:)`) with the line break that follows, although the selection's text stops before the break. `LineEndSelectionTests` asserts this on three lines (the first body line of `labels-multipage.pdf` page index 3, the first line of `two-column.pdf`'s left column, and the first body line of `rotated.pdf`'s `/Rotate 90` page) for a range to the line's end, the last word (as a long press selects it), the whole line, a drag from the line's first character to 6 pt past its last and two lines ending at a line's end, and on every line of `text-layout.pdf`'s rotated-text page except its last, which has no break after it. `ComposedSelectionTests` shows the same at the end of a selection across two pages and for a piece of a composed selection. A drag that ends further past a line's end does not always end there: on `two-column.pdf`, 40 pt past the left-column line selected part of the full-width title above it instead (recorded, not asserted). Stored as reported, the anchor quoted "water" but its range held "water\n", and it could not be restored (not found, or "Location needs review"; controls in both tests). The builder therefore drops trailing line breaks from a range while PDFKit selects the same text without them.
- `exact_text` is PDFKit's text for the whole selection. With one segment it equals the segment's text. With several, PDFKit composes it, and not with one fixed separator. In the shapes `ComposedSelectionTests` checks (pieces added with `PDFSelection.add` on one page and across two pages, and selections across pages), PDFKit joins pages with one "\n", puts pieces on one page next to each other with nothing between them, and keeps the line break after a piece that ends at a line's end when another piece follows on its page; that break is outside the stored ranges, so it is in no segment's text. The builder therefore checks a new anchor by restoring it, with the same extraction: each stored range is rebuilt into a selection on its page and the selections are combined in document order, exactly as the resolver's exact step does it. The anchor is refused (`quotationMismatch`) unless every range gives back its segment's text and the combined text is `exact_text`, scalar for scalar. An earlier version of this check required the segments' texts joined by "\n" to equal `exact_text`; that refused 6 of the 11 composed shapes tested, every one with two or more separate pieces on a page except where the expected "\n" happened to be the kept line break (gate review finding R4-01), and it would have accepted a selection claiming the "\n"-joined text, which its ranges cannot give back (`testSelectionWhoseRangesCannotGiveBackItsQuotationIsRefused`). A client on another platform restores segment by segment (each segment's `text` at its `text_range`) and keeps `exact_text` as the quotation; it does not rebuild `exact_text` from the segments.
- Quotation comparison is scalar-exact, not Swift `String ==` (which treats "é" and "é" as equal).

**Coordinates.** `line_rects` are PDFKit's per-line selection bounds (`selectionsByLine`, `bounds(for:)`), which are in PDF user space, normalized to the CropBox: `[x, y, w, h]` as fractions of the box, origin at the box's lower-left, y up, unrotated (the page's own coordinate system, before `/Rotate`), rounded to 6 decimals. `page_rotation` is the effective `/Rotate` (inherited) normalized to 0, 90, 180 or 270 degrees clockwise. A client on any platform draws a stored rectangle on the page as displayed, normalized to the displayed page with origin top-left, as:

| Rotation | Displayed rect |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | `(x, 1 - y - h, w, h)` |
| 90 | `(y, x, h, w)` |
| 180 | `(1 - x - w, y, w, h)` |
| 270 | `(1 - y - h, 1 - x - w, h, w)` |

This mapping agrees with PDFKit's `transform(for: .cropBox)` and with `PDFView.convert(_:from:)` on every rotation tested.

**Restoring.** In the order of plan 7.5: (1) same asset digest, same page text digest, the stored scalar range rebuilt into a selection whose text equals the stored text, and the combined selection equals `exact_text`: exact (the check capture makes before it stores an anchor). (2) Otherwise, within the same asset, the segment text with its exact stored prefix and suffix found exactly once: relocated. The reader draws a relocated highlight where its text is now, not at its stored rectangles, and saves a fresh anchor for it: one built by the capture path from the relocated selection (current page text digest, scalar range, context and line rectangles), accepted only if it quotes exactly the stored text at exactly the relocated ranges. The record keeps its ID, creation date, asset ID and quotation, and the next open restores it exactly (`HighlightRestorer`, which the harness runs on every open). Several matches, or matches of the bare text without its context: "Location needs review", not drawn. No match: not found, not drawn. In both cases the record, quotation included, stays exactly as stored. (3) A different asset digest is never relocated automatically (cross-edition needs confirmation).

**Highlights and overlays.** Highlights are Sylvia records (in the product, rows written in the capture transaction of plan 11.1; in the spike, an atomically written JSON file with `completeFileProtectionUntilFirstUserAuthentication`). They are never PDF annotations and the PDF file is never written. They are drawn by a `PDFDocument` subclass whose `pageClass` is a `PDFPage` subclass that, after drawing the page, draws the `ochre.tint` fill with multiply blending and an `ochre` underline in user space (`transform(_:for:)` then the stored rects). This is the same path PDFView uses for its tiles and that offscreen rendering uses. The page subclass must not have stored properties: PDFKit creates pages without running Swift initializers, so per-page overlay data lives in an associated object guarded by a mutex (PDFView draws tiles on background threads while the main thread changes highlights). Applying highlights touches only the pages that carry them or carried them, so opening a long document does not instantiate every page. PDFView keeps its rendered tiles when only the overlay changes; after a new highlight the reader calls `PDFView.setNeedsDisplay()`, which redraws the visible tiles through the overlay and keeps selection, page and scale, and dismisses the saved selection so its tint does not cover the highlight. Colours come from the design package in the product; the spike copies the plan 4.4.2 light values because PDF pages are drawn on white in both appearances.

**Links and actions.** The reader installs a `PDFViewDelegate` (`LinkInterceptor`), so PDFKit's default for URL links (opening the URL) never runs. What Sylvia decides, through the delegate:

- URL actions (`pdfViewWillClick(onLink:with:)`): `http`, `https` and `mailto` show the full URL and open only after the user confirms; every other scheme is blocked, including `file:` (PDFKit presents a `/Launch` action as a URL action whose URL is the launch file resolved against the PDF's location) and custom app schemes.
- /GoToR (`pdfViewOpenPDF(_:forRemoteGoToAction:)`), named Find (`pdfViewPerformFind`) and named GoToPage (`pdfViewPerformGo(toPage:)`): nothing happens (recorded as blocked).

What PDFView does by itself, with no delegate call, observed with the interceptor installed (`actions-nonurl.pdf`, `actions.pdf`): internal GoTo and named NextPage and LastPage navigate inside the document; ResetForm resets in-memory form field values to their defaults (the file is not written). JavaScript, SubmitForm and named Print do nothing on iOS: no page change, no delegate call, no change to the form field, nothing presented (no print sheet; the check is shown to report a presented alert and a pending print job, `NonURLActionTests.testControlPresentedUIDetectorSeesAnAlertAndAPrintJob`) and no URL. In `actions.pdf` the document-level JavaScript (which asks for a canary URL), the open action and the page-open action (canary URIs) do not run when the file is displayed and its pages change, with the delegate or without it (`LinkInterceptionTests`). `NonURLActionTests` asserts each of these outcomes, including that /GoToR, Find and GoToPage reach the delegate and nothing else does. Other relative named actions (PrevPage, GoBack, GoForward, ZoomIn, ZoomOut) were not in the fixtures and are assumed to be performed by PDFView like NextPage. None of these opens anything or leaves the document. `LinkPolicy.decide(action:in:)` states the same outcomes for every action type, so tests can compare; it is not called for the actions PDFView performs itself. Sylvia never calls `PDFView.perform(_:)` for an action from the file.

Chained and additional actions, outline and form fields (`actions-chained.pdf`). Every URI and JavaScript action in it aims at a canary URL the test harness records if the system ever opens it, except link D's https URI; the other actions are GoTo and Named actions inside the file and a /Launch of /bin/sh. Its open action and page 0's open trigger are GoTo actions to page index 2 (each followed by a /Next canary), so running either would show as the view leaving page 0. What follows was observed for the shapes in that file on the iOS 26.5 simulator, with Sylvia's delegate and, as a control, without it: `PDFView.perform(_:)` on every action PDFKit presents (`ChainedActionTests`) and real taps on every link and field (`LinkTapUITests`). Other shapes are untested.

- **/Next.** PDFKit performs only the first action of a chain and never an action that /Next names: a dictionary after GoTo, NextPage, an https URI and a canary URI, an array (a URI, a /Launch and a JavaScript action) and a cycle, with the delegate. Without it, the same holds for the dictionary chains after GoTo, NextPage and the canary URI (only the first URL opens) and for the array chain, performed and tapped, and for the cycle, tapped. The https chain was run only with the delegate, because without it PDFKit's default would open the https URL in Safari.
- **/AA of a link without /A.** When the /AA is {U} or {U, E}, PDFKit presents the mouse-up action as the link's own: a tap reaches `pdfViewWillClick(onLink:with:)` and this policy like any URL link, and without the delegate PDFKit's default opens it. When the /AA also or only holds mouse-down ({D, U}, {U, D}, {D}), PDFKit presents no action and a tap runs nothing, with or without the delegate. A link with an /A and every /AA trigger presents its /A.
- **Form fields.** PDFKit presents no action for a text field merged with its widget whose /AA declares every annotation and field trigger, or for a widget whose separate field dictionary holds the triggers (keystroke, format, validate and calculate, and also mouse-down, mouse-up, focus and blur). A separate widget whose own /AA holds only mouse-up has that action presented as its own: `PDFView.perform` sends it to the delegate (blocked here) and, without the delegate, opens it. A real tap on that field starts editing instead, and tapping each of the three fields, typing a digit and leaving it changes only the field's value and runs nothing, with or without the delegate.
- **Outline.** PDFKit presents each outline item's /A as `PDFOutline.action` (an item with only a /Dest as a GoTo). Performed with the delegate, the URI item reaches `pdfViewWillClick` and is blocked, the GoTo item navigates without running its /Next, and the JavaScript child does nothing; without the delegate the URI item is opened. A table of contents in the product therefore navigates to an item's destination and sends any other action through `LinkPolicy`; it never performs an outline action while the delegate is not installed.
- **Document, page, link and field triggers.** Opening and displaying the file, changing pages, setting the three fields' values and closing the file run none of the document (/WC, /WS, /DS, /WP, /DP), page (/O, /C), link or field triggers, with or without the delegate, and the document JavaScript name tree does not run. For the open action and page 0's open trigger this is seen as the view staying on page 0 when the file is displayed and every time page 0 is shown again (`ChainedActionTests`, with and without the delegate); for the others, as their canaries never arriving and nothing reaching the delegate. Sylvia never saves or prints a PDF, so the save and print triggers have nothing to run on. Pointer-only triggers (mouse enter and exit, on an iPad with a trackpad) were not exercised; that is a device step.

`PDFActionAudit` lists, for diagnostics only, the actions declared in these places: the catalog's /OpenAction and /AA; every entry of the document JavaScript name tree (/Names /JavaScript, through /Kids); every outline item's /A (through /First and /Next); every form field's /A and /AA (/AcroForm /Fields, through /Kids; a field merged with its widget is listed once, with its page); each page's /AA; and each annotation's /A and /AA. It follows every action's /Next. It does not read XFA form scripts, presentation steps (/PresSteps /NA and /PA), a link's /PA, scripts held in media, rich-media or 3D dictionaries, embedded files, or actions in objects none of those places refers to. A name-tree node, outline item or form field reached a second time (a cycle or a shared node) is reported once and not followed again, and so is a /Next that returns to an action already on the current chain. A /Next action reached along two different paths is not a cycle and is listed again on each path, so /Next arrays that name the same action several times at every level give a small file exponentially many rows (a 30-action file in `testAuditStopsOnAnExponentialNextGraph`). The audit therefore stops following a chain or tree after 64 levels and stops after 4,096 rows in all. Nothing Sylvia enforces depends on it.

**Search.** "Search this book" runs over Sylvia's stored page text and returns scalar ranges; a hit becomes an anchor through the same builder and reopens through the same resolver. Image-only pages never produce hits.

## Consequences

- PDF anchors are portable without PDFKit: page index, scalar range into recorded extracted text, and geometry that any renderer can draw with the table above. Another platform must reproduce page text to use `text_range`; when its extractor differs, `page_text_sha256` detects it and re-anchoring falls back to exact text plus context, then review.
- An OS update can change `PDFPage.string`. The extraction version and page digests make that visible; re-extraction plus re-anchoring must run after an OS change before ranges are trusted. Between the iOS 26.5 and 27.0 simulator runtimes the page text of every page of the fixtures did not change (32 pages of the ten that open; compared by the drop-in).
- The page subclass is coupled to PDFKit's page-instantiation behaviour (no Swift initializers) and to PDFKit drawing it on background threads. These tests must stay in the product suite: `GeometryOverlayTests.testOverlayPagesAreTheSubclass` and the overlay pixel tests, `PDFKitObservationTests.testPageClassInstancesSkipSwiftPropertyInitializers`, `OverlayConcurrencyTests` (both tests, and their Thread Sanitizer run), and `OverlayRedrawTests`.
- The link and action handling relies on PDFKit behaviour observed on the iOS 26.5 simulator: which actions reach the delegate, that /Next is never performed, which /AA triggers PDFKit presents as an annotation's action, and which outline actions it presents. These tests pin that behaviour and must stay in the product suite, so an OS release that changes it fails a test instead of silently changing what a PDF can trigger: `LinkInterceptionTests`, `NonURLActionTests` (with its control for the "nothing presented" check), `ChainedActionTests` (with its controls without the delegate) and `LinkTapUITests` (with its control without the interceptor).
- Anchors depend on PDFKit reporting a selection that ends at a line's end with the line break in its range, which the builder removes. `LineEndSelectionTests` pins both the behaviour and the fix and must stay in the product suite; if PDFKit stops adding the break, the test shows it and the trimming does nothing. The builder does not depend on how PDFKit composes a multi-piece selection's text, because it checks each new anchor by restoring it; `ComposedSelectionTests` pins that composition for the shapes tested, shows the check accepts them and refuses a selection whose ranges cannot give back its quotation, and must stay in the product suite with it.
- PDFView caches rendered tiles; a new highlight needs `setNeedsDisplay()`. Its cost on a device was not measured.
- Text drawn with a rotated text matrix on an unrotated page (generated fixture `text-layout.pdf`: upward, downward, upside down and 30 degrees) is extracted line by line and its anchors restore exactly, but the overlay is wrong for it. A line's rectangle is PDFKit's axis-aligned line box and the underline is drawn along its bottom edge, which is the baseline side only for upright text: on vertical lines it is a block 25 to 30 pt long over the line's first or last glyphs, on upside-down lines it runs along the tops of the glyphs, and a 30-degree line's box covers most of the next line's (`text-layout-rotated.txt`, `overlay-rotated-text.png`). PDFKit gives each line's reading direction (the boxes of its first and last characters, which `TextLayoutTests` checks against the drawing direction), so storing a direction per line would let the overlay draw the underline on the baseline side of vertical and upside-down lines. Slanted lines would need quadrilaterals, which PDFKit's selection API does not give. Decision: contract consolidation and M3 (Open items; the approach, a reading direction per line, was approved by the product owner on 22 October 2026 with A54). `TextLayoutTests` pins the current behaviour.
- PDFKit orders page text by its own layout analysis, not by the content stream: two columns drawn in a scrambled order come out in reading order; a column drawn from its bottom line up comes out in reading order but with no break between its first two lines ("order.Bottom-up"), so search across that break with a space finds nothing; two downward lines come out in the reverse of their reading order (`text-layout-order.txt`, `text-layout-rotated.txt`). Anchors index that text, so they still restore exactly; quotations and search see what PDFKit extracted.
- Owner-password PDFs that forbid copying get no quotation, search or AI text if the proposal is accepted. *(Accepted by the product owner on 22 October 2026, PO-05 (a): a narrowing of plan 9.2's supported experience that no failed spike forced.)*
- The truncation check looks for `%%EOF` in the last 1,024 bytes. A valid file with more than 1 KB of junk after its last `%%EOF` would be reported as truncated; an incrementally updated file cut just after an earlier `%%EOF` passes the check and is then judged by PDFKit's parser. Neither case is in the fixtures.

## Alternatives considered

- **Write highlights as PDF annotations.** Rejected by plan 9.2 and I-08: it modifies the user's file and ties Sylvia's record to a third-party format.
- **`PDFPageOverlayViewProvider` overlay views.** Not chosen: a separate view cannot multiply its fill with the page, so the fill would cover glyphs or need transparency that changes the token colour, and it needs its own coordinate mapping. The page subclass draws in the same pass as the page.
- **`PDFView.highlightedSelections`.** Draws PDFKit's own highlight colour with no underline; it cannot carry the Ochre fill-plus-underline that survives grayscale.
- **Store UTF-16 offsets in the portable anchor.** Rejected by plan 7.5; they stay in `reader_locator`.
- **Text range only, no geometry.** Loses position when text extraction changes and cannot draw on another platform; rectangles are cheap to store.
- **Third-party PDF engines.** Not needed for anything tested; they add a license and a dependency for no gained capability.
