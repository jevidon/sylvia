## Context

Section 10 asks for one application-owned playback service, file-local audio anchors with a derived global timeline for multi-file editions, durable bounded excerpts with recorded actual timing, and a preview that never corrupts progress. Section 3.1 makes the codec/container list a proposed narrowing: only combinations that pass playback, seek, chapter and excerpt tests ship. Section 19.2 proposes budgets: start/resume p95 1.5 s, capture preview within 500 ms of the intended boundary, crash position loss at most 5 s, acknowledged capture save p95 200 ms.

Stage 0 had no physical device. Everything below that is measured was measured on the **simulator (iPhone 17, iOS 26.5 runtime 23F77, on an Apple M5 Max host)** unless marked otherwise; device behaviour is pending. The measurements of rounds 3 to 6 were taken with every test player at 1 % output volume (the Mac's speakers are shared; the product owner accepted 1-2 %). The volume is applied after decoding, the time-pitch stage and the analysis taps, and the media clock is unaffected (`audio-output.txt`). Muting the players (`AVPlayer.isMuted`) was not used: it changed what was rendered after the time-pitch stage at 2x and 3x (G0.4 report row 34). The engines' own default, and the product's, is full volume. The host is shared with other jobs' simulators (2-8 booted during the round-4 clean run, 2-4 during round 5's, 1-2 during each of round 6's two), and some timings grow with its load, the first precise seek into an MP3 most of all: since round 4 every timing table records the host's load averages at its start and end, the cold-resume and capture tables per trial (`load_1m`), and the CI drop-in samples them every 30 s (`host-load.tsv`; review R4-02); since round 5 the open-time table also records the time and the load at each row's start and end and takes the median of three opens per file, and the remux table records the load per row (review R5-05).

## Decisions for the product owner

Agent-proposed; nothing below is approved (review R6-04: these choices had been stated only inside long decisions and table cells). The G0.4 report lists the same decisions with their evidence rows (PO-6, the quick-capture narrowing, is ADR-0007's).

- **PO-1, release codec list** (plan 3.1, D05; final only after the device run): plan 3.1's commitment plus whichever of FLAC (in MP4, native), Opus (in Ogg, MP4, CAF) and WAV (no chapters) the device passes; Opus in WebM is out. Proposed: every format the device passes, with its limits under PO-3 if the remux is not adopted. Evidence: "Provisional release codec list" below.
- **PO-2, per-file timing mode** (decision 2): estimated timing only for an MP3 the import proves constant bit rate, precise timing for everything else; or precise timing for everything. Proposed: the per-file mode.
- **PO-3, import remux** (decision 17) for the files that stay precise (VBR MP3, native FLAC, Ogg Opus): remux and keep the original as the asset of record (storage doubles), remux and keep only the copy, or no remux with each format's length or depth limit stated. With a remux: every VBR MP3 or only long ones. Proposed: a remux (storage choice to the product owner), VBR MP3 only when about an hour or longer.
- **PO-4, mono excerpts** (decision 13): mono AAC, a stereo source averaged; or stereo excerpts. Proposed: mono.
- **PO-5, playback control defaults** (decisions 18-21): skip intervals (spike default 15 s both ways), the sleep-timer kinds, the rewind after a long pause (proposed: offered as a setting, off by default).

## Decision

### Playback service

1. One `PlaybackService` per app (`@MainActor`), owning the edition timeline, the engine queue, checkpoints, interruption policy and capture preview. The engine is an `AVQueuePlayer` that holds the current member and the next available one, so member boundaries play without a gap and without re-buffering. Nothing else in the app plays the book. The service also owns the other plan 10.1 controls: skip intervals, the sleep timer, the rewind after a long pause and the incomplete-edition warning (decisions 18-21).
2. **Per-file timing mode** (review R4-01). The import decides once per file how the player, the preview and the excerpt export open it, and stores the decision with the member (`AssetTiming`, `EditionMember.timing`, `TimingDecision`):
   - **Estimated timing** (no `AVURLAssetPreferPreciseDurationAndTimingKey`) for an MP3 the import proves constant bit rate (below). AVFoundation then derives a position from the bit rate, which is exact for such a file, and opens and seeks at once at any depth. On the simulator: decoder windows at 23 positions in six proved files (the 10-minute CBR and ID3-CHAP encodes, the CBR encode without its Info frame, the real 22.05 kHz LibriVox MP3, a book member, and the 10-hour CBR file up to 10.2 h in) were sample-identical to the precise decodes and at the same place against the master (13.1 ms decoder offset for the 44.1 kHz encodes, 0.0-0.1 ms for the LibriVox file; `estimated-timing-decoder.tsv`); every cold resume through the playback service (33 trials) landed exactly where precise timing lands (difference 0.0 ms) and took 0.14-0.15 s at every depth from 20 minutes to 10 h into the 10-hour file (0.21-0.22 s in the LibriVox file, 0.14-0.15 s in a book member), against 0.47-0.54 s at 20 minutes rising to 9.2-12.3 s at 10 h with precise timing, under host loads of 6.8-32.5 in round 6's final clean run (round 4's clean run, at loads of 11-74: the same 0.14-0.15 s, against 0.48 s and 9.5-12.8 s) (`budgets.txt`); a 30 s capture export 1, 5 and 9.5 h into the 10-hour file took 0.07 s cold (with precise timing 15.5 s 9.5 h in, `long-formats-capture.tsv`), a pending preview 9.5 h in started in 0.13 s, and captures and previews across the four-file book's members were exact (at most 13.1 ms at head, seam and tail; `estimated-timing-capture.tsv`); member boundaries, and seeks into other members at 0.5x, 1x and 2x, rendered the same content as with precise timing (`real-engine-boundary.txt`, `real-engine-seek-rate.txt`).
   - **Precise timing** for every other file. Without it AVFoundation lands seconds or minutes off: VBR MP3 with a Xing header 0.56 s for the player and 0.17-1.63 s for the decoder in a 10-minute file, and 1.5 s, 16.3 s and 129.4 s for the decoder 3.4, 8.3 and 9.5 h into a 10-hour file (1.5 s and 14.1 s for the player at 3.4 and 8.3 h); VBR MP3 without a header 1.7 s; Ogg Opus 2.0 s; native FLAC: in the 10-minute file, which has no seek table (its metadata blocks are STREAMINFO, VORBIS_COMMENT and PADDING; the codec matrix's FLAC row was measured on it), 2.9 s for the player, and the decoder's windows were not found within ±30 s of the target; in the 3- and 10-hour files, which have a seek table with a point every 10 s (1,081 and 3,675 points), as the reference encoder writes one, the audio at the requested position was not found within ±150 s of it in any of 5 decoder and 4 player windows (normalized correlation at most 0.33, against 0.98-1.00 wherever timing is right) (`codec-matrix.tsv`, `long-seek-latency.txt`, `estimated-timing-decoder.tsv`). With the option every format lands within 70 ms of the target (126 ms for the first rendered audio of the real LibriVox MP3). It has a cost that depends on the container and grows with the file (review R3-01; every provisional format measured at 10 minutes and 10.2 hours, native FLAC and Ogg Opus also at 3 hours, each on a fresh copy; `long-formats-open.tsv`): creating an `AVPlayerItem` for a new native FLAC file scans it, about 2.7-3.4 s per hour of file over six runs (in round 6's final clean run, each the median of three opens, 0.45 s for 10 minutes, 9.0 s for 3 hours and 31.2 s for 10.2 hours, with a seek table every 10 s as the reference encoder writes one); an MP3 without a Xing/VBRI header about 0.8-1.0 s per hour (0.14 s for 10 minutes, 9.2 s for 10.2 hours); Ogg Opus about 0.09-0.16 s per hour (0.52 s for 3 hours; 1.57 s for 10.2 hours as .ogg, 1.03 s as .opus). MPEG-4 (AAC-LC, HE-AAC v1 and v2, ALAC, FLAC, Opus), CAF, WAV and MP3 with a header or CBR open in at most 0.026 s at every length. Without the option every format opens in at most 0.027 s, so the scan is the option's. The cost is paid on every launch (a relaunch creates a new item), by every preview and by every excerpt export from such a file (a 30 s export 9.5 h into the 10.2-hour native FLAC took 29.1 s, `long-formats-capture.tsv`); an MP3 opened with precise timing has a second cost, a first seek proportional to its depth into the file (Consequences), which the per-file mode removes for a proved-CBR MP3. Both are budget failures, not errors: the files open, play and export correctly (decision 7); for the files that stay precise, the import remux of decision 17 removes both.
   - **How the import proves a constant bit rate** (`MP3FrameScanner`; `MP3FrameScannerTests`, `mp3-cbr-detection.tsv`). It walks every MPEG audio frame header from the first frame (after any ID3v2 tags) to the end of the audio (before an APEv2 and/or ID3v1 tag) and proves the file constant only if every frame is Layer III with the same MPEG version, sample rate, bit rate and channel count; each header sits exactly where the previous frame ends (no junk, no resynchronisation, no tag inside the audio, no free-format or truncated frame, nothing unknown after the audio); the first frame carries LAME's `Info` tag or no tag; the `Info` tag's frame count equals the frames walked; and the scanned audio duration agrees with AVFoundation's precise duration within three frames. `Info` and `Xing` are the same structure (flags, frame count, byte count, a 100-entry table of contents, and LAME's extension with the encoder delay and padding); LAME writes `Info` for a constant and `Xing` for a variable or average bit rate. The name is a claim, not proof: the frames decide, so an `Info` tag on a variable stream proves nothing, and a `Xing` or `VBRI` tag keeps the file precise even if its frames share one bit rate (a `Xing` table of contents is what estimated seeks in a VBR file follow). The walk reads one 4-byte header per frame: 0.20 s for the 294 MB 10-hour file (Debug build), part of the import's inspection. Proved: the eight CBR fixtures (all with LAME `Info` tags) and two variants (the `Info` frame removed; an ID3v1 tag appended). Not proved: the four VBR fixtures (9 bit rates each; two with a `Xing` tag) and three variants (one 80 kb/s frame spliced into the CBR file, 100 zero bytes appended, a second MP3 appended, which puts an ID3 tag inside the audio). The unit tests cover the other rules (a `Xing` or `VBRI` tag on a constant stream, an `Info` tag on a variable one, a wrong `Info` frame count, a changing sample rate or channel count, free-format and Layer II frames, truncation, malformed tags) and 900 mutated or random inputs, none proved.
   - **If a variable-bit-rate file were misdetected** and opened with estimated timing, its positions would be off by seconds to minutes (0.2-129 s in the VBR files above): a resume, a preview or an excerpt export would play or keep the wrong audio, and the excerpt's recorded actual range would not show it (it is the decoder's claim). The stored anchors (file-local milliseconds) stay correct, so reopening the file with precise timing corrects every later use, but an excerpt already exported would hold the wrong audio. The scan cannot accept a variable stream, because it requires the same bit rate in every frame, which is what a constant bit rate is; what remains is a defect in the parser or a file that AVFoundation reads differently from the scan, which the duration check catches when the two count different frames. A file the scan cannot prove costs only speed.
3. Pitch is preserved with `AVAudioTimePitchAlgorithm.timeDomain` (suited to speech). Rates 0.5x-3x; a 1 kHz tone stayed at 999.8-999.9 Hz at 0.5x, 1x, 2x and 3x, with `varispeed` as the control (500-3000 Hz) proving the measurement point (`rate-pitch.txt`).
4. Audio session: category `.playback`, mode `.spokenAudio`, route-sharing policy `.longFormAudio`. It is configured and activated only by `play()` and by preview. Inspection, decoding, import, capture and excerpt export never touch it (test with a sentinel category: `audio-session.txt`). It is released with `setActive(false, options: .notifyOthersOnDeactivation)` whenever playback stops for good, so an app whose audio our playback interrupted is told it may resume: a user pause (including the remote pause command), the end of a preview that does not resume main playback, the end of the edition, a stop at a blocked or failed segment, and loading another edition (after stopping whatever the outgoing edition was playing, a capture preview included; decision 16). It is not released after an interruption (the system has already deactivated it, and the service resumes when the system says so) or after the output device is lost (headphones removed: another app must not start on the speaker), nor around a reorder that keeps playing (review R2-06; `PlaybackServiceTests` with a spy session; on the simulator the real deactivation after a pause succeeds, `audio-session.txt`, and every per-format preview released it, `preview-end-formats.tsv`). Whether another app actually resumes is device checklist step 15. Trade-off to watch on the device: after a release, the system may hand the Now Playing controls to the app that resumes.
5. Remote commands: play, pause, toggle, skip back/forward (by the listener's intervals, 15 s by default, advertised to the system; decision 18), change position, change rate (0.5-3x), previous/next track mapped to chapter navigation. Seek-by-holding, like, dislike, rating, bookmark, repeat, shuffle, language options and stop are explicitly disabled (`isEnabled` defaults to true). Now Playing carries title, duration, elapsed time, rate, default rate, chapter count and current chapter. No transport command ever captures (plan 10.5; quick capture is ADR-0007). A command reports `.success` to the system only when the service acts on it (review R4-03): play that cannot start (a blocked or failed segment, a failed session activation), a play/pause that changed nothing and next chapter in the last chapter report `.commandFailed`, and any command with nothing loaded `.noActionableNowPlayingItem`. During a capture preview (decision 16) Now Playing describes the preview (the book's title, the preview's own elapsed time and length, rate 1, no chapter fields); pause and play/pause stop the preview; play is accepted (the preview is what plays); skip, scrubbing, rate and previous/next track are disabled while the preview runs, so the system can show them unavailable, and refused with `.commandFailed` if one arrives, because a preview must leave the book where it was. Tested over fakes (`RemoteCommandTests`: every command during a preview, its status and what Now Playing shows) and with the real engine and the shared command center on the simulator (`now-playing.txt`: during a preview the published duration is the preview's 30 s, the rate 1, the six navigation commands disabled, skip forward refused, play/pause stopped the preview with the book paused at its saved point and the session released). What the Lock Screen and headphones do during a preview is device checklist step 7.3.
6. Interruptions and routes: on `interruption began` the service pauses and remembers whether the user was playing; on `ended` it resumes only when the system passes `.shouldResume` and the user was playing. A route change with reason `oldDeviceUnavailable` pauses; no route change ever starts playback.
7. Error states: a member not on this device blocks only its segment (`segmentUnavailable(memberIndex)`); a member the library lists but whose file is gone is `failed(missingFile)`; a member that does not decode is `failed(decodeError)`. Playback elsewhere in the edition is unaffected, and `play()` never claims playing over a blocked segment. Opening a member is not an error state however long it takes: the engine waits for the item with no time limit, and only the item's own failure is `failed(decodeError)`. While a member opens (load, a seek into another member, a reorder, the return from a preview) the service reports `preparing(memberIndex)`, its position is the target point (a checkpoint written meanwhile records it), `play()` records the wish to play without activating the audio session, a pause cancels the wish, and playback starts when the member is ready; a seek during the open replaces it (review R3-01: the earlier fixed 20 s limit reported a valid 7-hour FLAC as undecodable; in round 6's final clean run the 10.2-hour FLAC opened after 28.0-30.3 s on the simulator (6 cold resumes; 28.5-31.6 s in round 6's first, 27.1-27.3 s in round 5's, 27.8-30.4 s in round 4's, 32.9-35.4 s in round 3's), the status passing through `preparing`, and played from the checkpoint; every one of the 66 cold resumes in the long files of every format ended `paused` at the checkpoint, none `failed`; `PreparingStateTests`, `long-formats-cold-resume.tsv`). The product shows this state to the user; the vocabulary has no key for it yet (open question for G0.6).

### Positions and progress

8. Checkpoints are durable writes (temporary file, `F_FULLFSYNC`, rename, directory sync) of the file-local last position, the separate furthest position, the listening total, the timeline revision and, since review 6, the wall-clock time of the write (for the rewind after a long pause across a relaunch, decision 20). They are written every 5 s of wall time or 4 s of media time while playing, checked at every player tick (every 0.5 s of media; with 1 s ticks the soak test once measured a 5,001 ms gap at 3x), so 3x playback still loses at most about 4.5 s of book, and on pause, seek, interruption, route change, item change, reorder, blocked segment, preview start, loading another edition (`editionChange`: the outgoing edition's last point, so switching books loses nothing of the one left; review R5-02) and lifecycle transitions (becoming inactive, entering the background, and termination when the system announces it with `willTerminateNotification`; a crash or SIGKILL announces nothing, so the periodic checkpoint bounds the loss). The unit tests' fake engine ticks like the real one, at every multiple of 0.5 s of media (exactly; the real engine adds main-thread latency), and they assert the bound: consecutive checkpoints 4.0-4.5 s of media apart at 3x, and a crash loss of at most 4.5 s of media in 80 random sessions at 0.5x-3x (review R4-05; `PlaybackServiceTests`).
9. The furthest position (plan 12, "furthest comparable position") is derived from observed contiguous main playback, never from a seek. Playback is tracked as listening runs: a run starts at load and at every seek, skip or chapter jump. A run that starts at or behind the frontier (within 1.5 s) moves the frontier as it plays past it. A run that starts ahead of the frontier becomes the frontier once it has been listened to contiguously for 30 s of media time, and then keeps moving it. Consequences: a 15 s skip, an intro skip or a jump to the next chapter followed by normal listening does not pin progress (the first fix of this rule did, review R1-02); a jump alone, or a jump followed by less than 30 s of listening (for example seeking to the last seconds of a book, T28), never moves it; going back never lowers it; and after 30 s of listening past a forward jump, the skipped part lies behind the frontier, so the furthest position is a place reached by listening, not proof that everything before it was heard. Completion is therefore never inferred from it (plan 12: explicit or confirmed). Listening time counts wall time of main playback only, and only while the media advances (plan 12: observed playback intervals): each interval between ticks adds the media advance divided by the rate, never more than the wall time that passed, so a stall while the service is playing (the seconds-long first seek of a cold MP3 resume, buffering) adds nothing; an interval in which the position jumped on its own adds nothing either (review R2-02; `PlaybackServiceTests.listeningTotalsExcludeStallsWithoutMediaProgress`; the soak's total matches the wall time from its first audio, not from `play()`, `soak.txt`).
10. AVQueuePlayer posts `didPlayToEndTime` before it makes the next item current, so the item-change checkpoint names the next member explicitly (found by the real-engine boundary test in this spike).

### Timeline and member order

11. Canonical anchors are asset-local milliseconds (`AudioPoint`); a selection that crosses files is an ordered list of file-local half-open ranges (`AudioRange`). The global timeline is derived from the confirmed member order and each member's precise duration, for display and seeking only, and carries a revision number. A reorder creates revision n+1; stored captures keep their file-local ranges unchanged.
12. Order proposal at import (plan 8.3): embedded disc and track numbers when every file has a unique pair, otherwise natural file-name order (digit runs compare numerically). Any disagreement between tags and names, missing or duplicate track numbers, or absent tracks (gaps, or a stated total larger than the files present) makes confirmation mandatory before the edition is ready. The order preview is always shown. Track numbers and totals are untrusted tag text (an ID3 `TRCK` frame can state any decimal), so absent tracks are reported as ranges derived from the numbers present, in time and memory proportional to the number of files (review R5-03: the earlier code listed every absent number up to the stated total, so one tag stating a huge total made the proposal's time and memory unbounded; `TimelineTests.T14_absentTracksFromUntrustedTagsAreBoundedRanges`).

### Excerpts

13. Export path: decode each file-local range with `AVAssetReader` (time range, trimmed by presentation timestamp) to mono Float32 at 44.1 kHz, concatenate the ranges in order, and encode **AAC-LC 64 kb/s mono 44.1 kHz in .m4a** through `AVAudioFile`. Write to a temporary file, sync, `rename(2)` over the target (atomic), sync the directory. Maximum 120 s per excerpt; memory is bounded by that limit. Stereo sources are downmixed to mono (speech; PO-4) by averaging the two channels, (L + R) / 2 (review R6-05; `PCMDecoder`): a source the system reports with two or more channels is decoded to two (the system's downmix for more than two) and averaged; a source it reports with one (mono, and HE-AAC v2, whose core is one channel plus parametric stereo) is decoded as one, at unity. Until review 6 the decode asked AVAssetReader for one channel, and the system converts two channels to one as an equal-power sum, (L + R) x 0.707: a file with the same speech in both channels, the usual stereo audiobook, came out 3 dB louder than it is (gain 1.414), above full scale for a source mastered within 3 dB of it (the LibriVox masters peak at -2.4 and -3.9 dBFS). The average keeps that speech at its level (gain 0.998, peak ratio 0.991; the dual-mono HE-AAC v2 matrix file 0.986) and never exceeds the louder channel; two different signals each keep half their level (least-squares weights 0.43-0.50 per channel across the 13 discrete-stereo formats). Measured on the simulator: `stereo-channels.tsv`, `StereoChannelTests` (G0.4 report row 37).
14. The capture record stores the requested ranges and the actual exported ranges (the decoder's timestamps of the first and last exported samples), the frame count, sample rate and the output file's duration. Measured against the PCM masters by cross-correlation, the stored actual start is off from the true content position by a constant 13.1 ms for the 44.1 kHz MP3 encodes (relative to the master they were encoded from; 0.1 ms for the real LibriVox MP3, whose own ffmpeg decode is the master) and 47.9 ms for HE-AAC sources, and by at most 1 ms for AAC-LC, ALAC, FLAC, Opus and WAV (`excerpt-timing-single-file.tsv`). All are well inside the 500 ms budget, so no per-codec correction is applied in v1. The excerpt record keeps each range's source format (`sourceFormats`, codec/sample rate/channels of the source track, for example `.mp3/44100/1`), so a per-codec correction can be applied to stored captures later. The spike's record does not carry the asset digest: the spike has no importer that computes one; the product anchor must (plan 7.5, see Open items).
15. Capture transaction (plan 10.2, 11.1): the anchor and note are written durably first and returned ("Moment saved · preserving audio"); the export runs after it and its outcome is a second durable write ("Audio excerpt saved", or "Excerpt not preserved · Retry" with the bookmark and note intact). One export per capture runs at a time; an export whose range was edited meanwhile is discarded and the edited range exported next. Removing a source while an excerpt is pending or failed requires an explicit choice; retained excerpts stay playable after the source is removed (T23). The export runs only in the process that made the capture, so a capture can be left pending by termination or a crash between the anchor write and the export result. At launch the app finds every audio capture that is pending with no export running in this process and exports it again (the harness does this right after loading the edition). A pending capture with no export running in this process offers "Retry" like a failed one; while an export for it runs, Retry is not offered, and a pending capture is never exported twice in parallel. Tested with a fake exporter and a reopened store (`CaptureTests`, `QuickCaptureTests`); an export actually cut off by a killed process was not reproduced (the 30 s export takes a few tenths of a second).
16. Preview (plan 10.3) plays in a separate `AVQueuePlayer`. Starting a preview pauses main playback, reads the paused position once, writes the preview-start checkpoint at that position and saves the context; preview never updates the position, furthest position, listening total or checkpoints; ending it (or an interruption or route loss during it) restores the saved context, and main resumes only if it was playing and the preview ended normally. A pause or play/pause during the preview, from the app or as a remote command (headphones, Lock Screen, Control Center), stops it (`stopPreview`, review R4-03): the saved context is restored paused even if main was playing before (the listener asked for silence), and the audio session is released as after any user pause; the other remote commands are refused during a preview (decision 5). A pending excerpt previews its source ranges: one queue item per range, in order, each ending at its range end with `forwardPlaybackEndTime`, so a capture across files plays the end of one member straight into the start of the next (every range after the first starts at 0 of its member, so no seek is needed between items). A range in a member that is not on this device has no preview; if an item fails to open, the preview ends and the main context is restored rather than skipping it silently. Loading another edition during a preview (review R5-02) stops the preview player first (before the fix it kept playing over the new edition, with the audio session released under it), writes the outgoing edition's last checkpoint at the saved main point with its progress untouched (`editionChange`), releases the session and loads the new edition paused; a late end of the old preview changes nothing (`PlaybackServiceTests.loadingAnotherEditionDuringAPreviewStopsThePreview`; on the real players, no preview audio reached the analysis tap more than 1 s after the load, `preview-load-edition.txt`).

### Import remux (proposed mitigation for the files that stay on precise timing; needs product-owner approval)

17. At import, copy the compressed packets of a member that stays on precise timing (decision 2) and whose opening or first deep seek grows with its length into a container with a packet table: native FLAC and Ogg Opus into MPEG-4 (.m4a), a VBR MP3 (with or without a Xing/VBRI header) into CAF (AVAssetWriter refuses MP3 packets in MPEG-4: `canAdd` is false for .m4a and .mp4 with each of the three 10-minute MP3 encodes, while CAF takes them with the source's duration; simulator, `ContainerProbeTests`, `container-probes.txt`; review R5-08). A proved constant-bit-rate MP3 needs no copy: the per-file timing mode already resumes it at once at any depth (decision 2). Nothing is decoded or re-encoded (`ImportRemuxer`: AVAssetReader without output settings into AVAssetWriter without output settings, temporary file, sync, rename). The player, preview and excerpt export then open the copy. Measured on the simulator in round 6's final clean run (`long-formats-remux.tsv`, with the host's load per row since round 5: 4.4-5.4): the remux of a 10.2-hour file took 13.0 s (native FLAC, 0.97 GB), 19.6 s (Ogg Opus) and 0.55-0.68 s (MP3, the CBR file included), and of a 3-hour file 3.6 s (FLAC) and 6.0 s (Ogg Opus); round 6's first clean run (tables at commit `eb7db57`) took 11.9 s, 19.7 s, 0.52-0.66 s, 3.6 s and 5.9 s, round 5's (`471b143`) 15.7 s, 19.7 s, 0.78-1.04 s, 3.2 s and 5.6 s, round 4's (`5d85b1f`) 15.1 s, 19.0 s, 0.85-1.18 s, 3.8 s and 7.2 s, round 3's (`943d5e7`) 14.7 s, 19.3 s, 1.0-1.4 s, 6.0 s and 10.1 s for the same files, an earlier run (`29bc5c4`) 99.4 s, 88.9 s, 1.0-23.5 s, 35.2 s and 27.0 s, and another (`0086b10`) 14.5 s, 20.1 s and 0.7-0.9 s for the 10.2-hour files, so the remux time varies widely between runs (11.9-99.4 s for the 10.2-hour FLAC, 0.5-23.5 s for a 10-hour MP3; cause not isolated; it is background import work, not on the playback path). ### The other plan 10.1 playback controls (review R6-02)

Plan 10.1 also asks for configurable skip intervals, a sleep timer, a rewind after a long pause and an incomplete-edition warning. (Its other items are decisions above: play and pause, rate with pitch, the chapter list and chapter navigation, decisions 1-5; elapsed and remaining time, which Now Playing publishes as the elapsed time and the duration, decision 5; selecting another edition, decision 16.) They were not in the gate's brief. They are decided here so that M4 builds them on rules that fit the position, checkpoint and session rules above; each is prototyped in the spike's `PlaybackService` and unit-tested over the fake engine and the manual clock (`PlaybackControlsTests`). Their defaults are product-owner decisions (PO-5 above).

18. **Skip intervals.** The service holds a backward and a forward interval, each a whole number of seconds from 5 to 120 (the spike's default 15 and 15; a value that is not a number keeps the default). The player's buttons and the remote skip commands use them, and Now Playing advertises them as the skip commands' preferred intervals (`MPSkipIntervalCommand.preferredIntervals`, refreshed on every update), so the Lock Screen and headphones skip by the listener's choice. A skip is a seek (decision 9: it never moves the furthest position by itself). Whether the Lock Screen shows a changed interval: device checklist step 7.4.
19. **Sleep timer**, of two kinds: after a duration of main playback (the countdown is wall time while the book plays, so a pause, an interruption or a capture preview does not use it up), or at the end of the chapter that plays (the member file when the edition has no chapters; after a seek, skip or chapter jump the stop moves to the end of the chapter then playing; a resume rewind keeps it). It is checked at every player tick (every 0.5 s of media, decision 8), so the end-of-chapter stop lands at most one tick, 0.5 s of media, past the chapter end (the product can stop exactly with a boundary time observer); a stop at a member boundary pauses at the start of the next member and its checkpoint names that member (decision 10). When it fires, main playback pauses with its own checkpoint (`sleepTimer`) and the audio session is kept, as after headphone removal (decision 4): an app whose audio the book interrupted is not told to resume while the listener sleeps, and the listener's next play or pause decides. Loading another edition clears it, and it is not kept across launches. Firing while the phone is locked, and the Lock Screen afterwards: device checklist step 17.
20. **Rewind after a long pause** (plan 10.1: "may rewind a configurable small amount, but should not mutate a saved bookmark or quote"). Off unless set. When set (a pause of at least N seconds rewinds M seconds), resuming main playback (the listener's play, a remote play command, or the system's resume after an interruption, decision 6) after main playback has been stopped for at least N seconds of wall time first moves back M seconds, clamped at the start of the edition. The pause is measured from when main playback stopped in this process, or, after a relaunch, from the wall-clock time stored with the last checkpoint (`savedAtUnixMs`, added in review 6; a checkpoint written before has none, and then no rewind follows the relaunch). The rewind is a seek with its own checkpoint (`resumeRewind`) and a new listening run, so the furthest position is never lowered (decision 9), and the replayed seconds count as listening. Captures and bookmarks are separate records and never change (asserted). There is no rewind when the listener has positioned the playhead since the pause (a seek, skip or chapter jump: the next play starts exactly there), after a capture preview (decision 16 restores the main context exactly), or at a blocked segment; a pause before the rewind's seek starts cancels it without moving anything. Device check: step 17.
21. **Incomplete-edition warning.** At load the service lists every member that cannot play here, by asset (`unavailableMembers`: not on this device, or a file the library lists that is gone; later also a file that turns out not to decode), and keeps the list current as members fail or open again; a reorder keeps the warning on the same members (`unavailableMemberIndices` gives their positions in the current order). Playback blocks only those segments (decision 7). The product shows the warning; the status vocabulary has no phrase for it, so the spike shows the proposed key `[audio.edition_incomplete]` (a design-package request; each member can say where it is with the existing `file.not_on_device`, "Not on this device · from {source}"). Not device-specific; no device step.

## Consequences

- Precise timing is mandatory for every file except a proved constant-bit-rate MP3 (decision 2), and it has two costs that grow with length (review R3-01, R3-02); the per-file timing mode removes both for a proved-CBR MP3 (review R4-01: 0.14-0.15 s cold at every depth to 10 h, in the clean runs of rounds 4 to 6). Resuming after a relaunch opens the member and seeks to the stored checkpoint, so a file on precise timing always pays both. (a) In an MP3 on precise timing the first seek in a file the process has not read grows with the checkpoint's depth into the file, by about 1 s per hour of depth on the simulator and more under host load (`budgets.txt`, 3 rounds at 8 depths from 20 minutes to 10 hours in each 10-hour MP3, the host's load per trial): 0.41-0.54 s at 20 minutes, 0.94-1.36 s at 1 h, 1.34-2.00 s at 1.5 h, 1.85-2.52 s at 2 h, 4.16-5.99 s at 5 h and 7.88-12.27 s at 10 h in round 6's final clean run (1-minute loads of 7.2-27.3 in those trials), against 0.40-0.45, 0.92-1.10, 1.30-1.61, 1.68-2.39, 4.04-6.03 and 8.06-10.06 s in round 5's, a lightly loaded one (4.6-16.7), and 0.42-0.48, 0.95-1.55, 1.36-2.19, 1.80-4.36, 4.32-8.17 and 8.27-12.76 s in round 4's at loads of 11-117; the 1.5 s budget is crossed between about 1.0 and 1.8 h into the file, depending on the run and the host's load (per round: CBR 1.11-1.46 h and VBR 1.26-1.61 h in round 6's final clean run; CBR 1.53-1.54 h and VBR 1.72-1.75 h in its first; CBR 1.39-1.51 h and VBR 1.73-1.76 h in round 5's; CBR 1.29-1.42 h and VBR 0.97-1.66 h in round 4's, whose slowest VBR round ran at loads of 33-117; CBR 1.42-1.50 h and VBR 1.22-1.77 h in a targeted run, `budgets-round4-targeted-run.txt`; CBR 1.23-1.31 h and VBR 1.25-1.57 h in round 3's clean run, 1.10-1.27 h and 1.42-1.48 h in an earlier run, single-trial runs 1.36-1.64 h, among them a Release build, `release-build-simulator.txt`). The round-4 review reported a run at load averages of 60-148 that crossed at 0.41-1.10 h (CBR) and 0.69-1.52 h (VBR) and exceeded the budget in 4 of 6 trials at 1 h (its tables are not in this folder). Depth claims for MP3 on precise timing are therefore indicative only. Short members are not affected: the real LibriVox section MP3 at 10 and 22 minutes and a 2.7-minute book member resumed within 0.7 s. (b) Opening a native FLAC, an MP3 without a header or an Ogg Opus file scans it, in proportion to the file's length and at every resume depth (decision 2): 28.1-30.4 s, 9.0-9.5 s and 0.99-1.60 s for a cold resume of the 10.2-hour files in round 6's final clean run (28.6-31.7 s, 8.1 s and 1.00-1.30 s in its first; 27.2-27.4 s, 7.8-8.1 s and 0.99-1.26 s in round 5's; 27.9-30.5 s, 8.4-8.7 s and 1.09-1.40 s in round 4's; 33.0-35.5 s, 9.5-9.9 s and 1.24-2.47 s in round 3's), and the scan also grows with the host's load (in the round-5 review's run a 10-minute native FLAC took 2.65 s to open during a load spike at 1-minute loads of 53-79, against 0.45-0.46 s in the three opens of round 6's final clean run and 0.49-0.50 s in round 5's; review R5-05). MPEG-4, CAF and WAV took at most 0.16 s at every depth up to 10 h. Once a file had been played in the same process, seeks took about 0.1 s; where the process had just read the whole file (the import's frame scan) before playing it, the first precise seek deep into an MP3 took 0.16-0.35 s instead of several seconds (G0.4 report row 21), which was not measured as a mitigation. Section 19.2's start/resume budget (p95 1.5 s, "excludes first codec inspection"; inspection happened at import, so a resume is not excluded) is therefore **met on the simulator at every depth for a proved-CBR MP3 with the per-file timing mode, and failed for every cold resume more than about 1-1.8 h into a VBR MP3 (shallower under host load), whether the book is one file or many, and at any position in a native FLAC file longer than about 25-31 minutes, an MP3 without a header longer than about 1.4-1.8 hours (both on a lightly loaded host; shorter under load) and, in some runs, an Ogg Opus file of about 10 hours**. The device numbers decide whether this holds on hardware (checklist steps 1 and 1.5). The mitigation measured for the files that stay precise is the import remux (decision 17): every remuxed copy resumed cold in 0.11-0.16 s. Whatever is chosen, the product shows `preparing` (decision 7) while a member opens.
- File-local anchors make reorders, missing members and source removal safe: nothing stored ever has to be rewritten.
- AAC .m4a excerpts are small (about 8 KB per second) and play everywhere Apple plays audio; they count toward retained-knowledge storage.
- Mono downmix loses stereo; acceptable for speech, to confirm with the product owner.
- The 5 s checkpoint rhythm costs one small durable write every 4-5 s while playing; the device run measures its battery cost (checklist step 3).
- Checkpoints are driven by the player's periodic time observer on the main thread. Anything that blocks the main thread for seconds delays them, and a crash in that window loses more than 5 s: the first soak run, whose own analysis ran on the main thread, saw a 14.1 s media gap between checkpoints at 3x (the analysis now runs off the main thread; `soak-earlier-runs.txt`). The product must keep heavy work (import inspection, export, search indexing) off the main thread while playing, or checkpoint from a timer that does not depend on it.

## Alternatives considered

- **AVPlayer with item replacement at member boundaries** instead of AVQueuePlayer: simpler, but each boundary would wait for the next item to load. Rejected.
- **AVAudioEngine with our own file scheduling:** sample-exact control and gapless joins, but re-implements buffering, route handling and Now Playing integration that AVPlayer provides. Not needed: the measured errors are tens of milliseconds.
- **Estimated timing (no precise key) for every file**, for speed: rejected on the measured errors (decision 2): seconds to minutes off in VBR MP3, native FLAC with or without a seek table, and Ogg Opus.
- **Precise timing for every file**, with the import remux as the only remedy for slow MP3 resumes (round 3's proposal): superseded for constant-bit-rate MP3 by the per-file timing mode (review R4-01), which is exact there, needs no second copy of the file and no decision about replacing the original, and resumes at once at any depth; the remux remains for the files that must stay precise.
- **A per-file timing mode without a full frame scan** (trusting the `Info` tag, or the first frame's bit rate): rejected; the tag is a claim, and one odd frame or appended file is enough to misplace positions (decision 2).
- **AVAssetExportSession passthrough excerpts:** keeps the source codec but cannot join ranges from different files or codecs into one file and needs a codec-specific container per source; rejected for one uniform AAC path.
- **Global-timeline anchors:** simpler arithmetic, but every reorder or member change would rewrite stored captures. Rejected by plan 7.5.
