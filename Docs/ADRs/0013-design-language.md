# ADR-0013: Design language (Marginalia) in the SylviaDesign package

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-10-06 (part 1); 2026-10-07 (part 2: components, snapshot tests, Sections 9 and 10; part 3: design catalog and interaction prototype, Section 11); 2026-10-12 (review round 1 fixes: lint, component contrast pairs, busy and pressed states, Monospace save time, unknown-kind label, `ochre_tint` conflict)
- **Deciders:** pending (design reviewer and iOS lead; product owner for the plan-text changes listed under Open items)
- **Plan references:** Sections 4.4 (normative), 6.2 (SylviaDesign row), 18.3, 20.1 (design conformance tests), 22.2 (CI contrast and literal checks), 22.4 (design-language ADR), Appendix A.1 and A.5; decision D15; tests T51, T53, T54; gate G0.6

## Context

Section 4.4 specifies Marginalia: color tokens in Paper and Night, three faces and eight type roles, a seven-marker set, a fixed status vocabulary, shape and motion values, and a grayscale and Increase Contrast conformance rule. Section 4.4.9 asks for a `SylviaDesign` package that owns all of it (asset catalog, type roles, marker mapping, vocabulary strings), a portable JSON export under `Contracts/design/`, a contrast check in CI and a lint that keeps literal colors, sizes and status phrases out of UI targets. Section 22.4 requires this ADR to record the final token values, Literata bundling and licensing, the Dynamic Type mapping and the grayscale conformance method. Gate G0.6 asks for the package at v0.1 and for every token pair's contrast to be verified in the asset catalog.

This ADR covers all three parts of G0.6: the package core, the Section 4.4.7 component library with its snapshot tests and the grayscale conformance method (Sections 9 and 10), and the design catalog app with the interaction prototype that Section 21.2 lists as an M0 deliverable (Section 11).

## Decision

### 1. One source, generated outputs

`Packages/Design` (swift-tools-version 6.2, Swift 6 language mode, iOS 26 and macOS 26) has four library and tool targets:

| Target | Role |

| `SylviaDesign` | The UI library (re-exports the tokens): the color asset catalog, the custom symbols, Literata, the String Catalog, and the SwiftUI/UIKit/AppKit API (`ColorToken.color`, `.sylviaFont(_:)`, `CustomSymbol.image`, `MarkerGlyph`, `Status.text()`). |

### 2. Color tokens

Literal values are the plan's (Section 4.4.2). The rules that produce the rest:

- **Derived dark tints.** Marker dark text composited at exactly 14% over Night (`#161512`), source-over in gamma-encoded sRGB, rounded to 8 bits per channel. `pine_tint` derives from Pine, `ochre_tint` (= Quote and Word tints) from Ochre.
- **Increase Contrast, "tints darken one step".** One step moves a tint 10% of the way toward its own marker text color of the same appearance (source-over of the text at 10% on the tint, gamma-encoded sRGB, 8-bit rounding). In Paper this darkens every tint. In Night the marker colors are lighter than the tints, so the same step strengthens the tint away from Night; taking "darken" literally there would move tints toward the ground and erase them, which is the opposite of what Increase Contrast is for. Flagged for design review.
- **Hairline.** Ink at 12% (Paper) and 14% (Night); 24% in both under Increase Contrast. Exact decimal alpha in the catalog; the export also gives `#RRGGBBAA` with alpha rounded to 8 bits.
- **Covers.** The five fields are appearance-invariant; the title is Paper (`#F4F2ED`) in every appearance. Night on the fields measures only 2.10 to 3.16:1, so "`ground` as the title color" can only mean Paper.
- **Shadow.** Paper ink at 10% in every appearance (a light shadow on Night would be a glow, excluded by "no colored shadows"; the boards draw `rgba(28,27,24,.10)`). Flagged for design review.
- **Aliases.** Quote and Word text are Ochre, Note text is Pine, Pending text is Muted; Quote and Word tints are the Ochre tint. Aliases always resolve to the root token, including overrides.
- **Increase Contrast leaves everything else unchanged** except the three recorded overrides below.

### 3. Contrast

Every text color that the plan permits on a fill is checked in all four appearances: ink, muted, Pine, Ochre and the seven marker text colors on `ground` and `surface`; `pine_on` on Pine; ink and the marker's own color on every tint ("Tint fills carry no text of their own except the marker's own color"); `cover_title` on every cover field: 44 pairs, 176 measurements. WCAG 2.x relative-luminance contrast; 4.5:1, or 3:1 only for pairs marked large (24 pt and up; none needs it). The threshold is never lowered.

It runs three ways: from the token definitions (Swift), from the compiled asset catalog read back through `UIColor`/SwiftUI on the simulator and host (Swift tests), and independently over the exported JSON in CI (`Scripts/lint-design.py contrast`, Python standard library).

**Adjustments.** All pairs pass in light and dark as specified. Under light Increase Contrast, darkening the tints left three marker texts below 4.5:1 on their own tint. Each was darkened by the documented minimal procedure (multiply each sRGB channel by 1 - k/100, smallest k at which every pair using the token passes); tests assert each override was needed and equals that procedure's result:

| Token (appearance) | Before | After | k | Failing pair before |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ochre` = Quote and Word text (light IC) | #8A5410 | #875210 | 2 | on IC quote tint: 4.38:1 |
| attention text (light IC) | #A63D2F | #9E3A2D | 5 | on IC attention tint: 4.20:1 |
| `muted` = Pending text (light IC) | #6B6760 | #646059 | 7 | on IC pending tint: 4.11:1 |

Flagged for design review. Lowest measured ratio after adjustment: 4.52:1 (attention text on its tint, light IC).

**Component pairs (review round 1).** The token pairs cover text on grounds and tints only. Components also draw text and glyphs where a token pair does not describe them, so `ComponentContrastPairs` (exported as `contrast.component_pairs`, with `minimum_ratio_non_text` 3) adds 14 pairs, measured in all four appearances from the token definitions, from the compiled asset catalog (host and simulator) and in Python over the export:

- the selection menu's action labels (the other appearance's ink) and Highlight glyph (the other appearance's Ochre) on its ink fill; the selected filter chip (ground on ink); the round play controls' glyphs (surface on the audio color, `pine_on` on Pine);
- "Saving…" on the busy primary button (below);
- every button role **pressed** and **disabled**, composited over the ground at the state's opacity.

Text needs 4.5:1, glyphs that carry meaning 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11; every glyph here also reaches 4.5:1). Two results changed components:

| State | Before | After | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pressed opacity (all roles) | 0.7 | **0.9** | At 0.7 pressed labels measured 3.23:1 (destructive, Paper) and 3.38:1 (primary). 0.9 is the smallest multiple of 0.05 at which every role's pressed label keeps 4.5:1 in every appearance (lowest 4.70). Tested as minimal (`pressedOpacityIsMinimal`). Flagged for design review: the press is subtler. |
| "Saving…" on the capture sheet's primary button | disabled fade (0.45): 2.07:1 light, 2.56:1 dark | **busy: full contrast** (6.70:1 light, 7.86:1 dark) | "Saving…" is a status the user must read (Section 4.4.2, I-03). The button is still disabled (not pressable; VoiceOver reads it as dimmed) but `sylviaButtonBusy` keeps it unfaded. |

**Disabled controls stay exempt, as a recorded design decision.** A disabled button is drawn at 0.45 opacity, which puts its label at 2.03 to 3.90:1 (`contrast-components-*.md`). That follows WCAG 1.4.3, which exempts inactive controls, and the table reports these rows as "below (disabled, exempt)" without failing. The rule that keeps this safe: **no vocabulary status may be shown on a disabled control**; a control whose label reports a running operation uses the busy state. The catalog's disabled controls (Capture and Save while an excerpt is being preserved, Send while the question is empty) carry action labels only. Design review: checklist step 21.

**Muted text is not permitted on tints.** It measures 4.29 to 4.64:1 on the Paper tints (below 4.5 on seven of eight; only the pending tint passes). Captions inside tinted blocks (for example the audio block's "No transcript") must use ink or the marker color.

### 4. Typography

**Literata bundling and licensing.** Literata 3.103 variable fonts (`Literata[opsz,wght].ttf` and the italic, renamed `Literata-Variable.ttf` and `Literata-Italic-Variable.ttf`; source, digests and the unmodified-file statement in `Fixtures/LICENSES.md`) ship in `Packages/Design/Sources/SylviaDesign/Resources/Fonts` under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, with `OFL.txt` beside them in the built bundle (the license must travel with the fonts). No Reserved Font Name is declared. `FontRegistration.registerLiterata()` registers both files for the process from the package bundle (`CTFontManagerRegisterFontsForURL`, scope `.process`); no Info.plist `UIAppFonts` entry is needed. Axes: `wght` 200-900, `opsz` 7-72. The release license inventory (ADR-0014) lists the font.

**Weights and optical size.** Literata roles are created with an explicit variation dictionary: `wght` = the role weight (400, 500, 600) and `opsz` = the rendered point size clamped to 7-72. Tests read the variation back from the created font (`CTFontCopyVariation`) at the default and largest sizes. System roles use the system font at the nearest system weight; values (timecodes, byte counts, digests) use SF Mono via `monospaced: true`.

**Dynamic Type mapping.**

| Role | Face, weight | Size / line at Large | Maps to | Size at AX5 (iOS 26.5) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Display | Literata 600, tracking -1.5% | 34 / 40 | Large Title | 60 |
| Title | Literata 600, tracking -1% | 28 / 34 | Title 1 | 58 |
| Heading | Literata 500 | 22 / 28 | Title 2 | 56 |
| Reading | Literata 400; user 14-28 | 19 / 30 | Body | 59.2 |
| Body | System 400 | 17 / 23 | Body | 53 |
| Callout | System 500 | 15 / 20 | Subheadline | 49 |
| Caption | System 400 | 13 / 18 | Footnote | 44 |
| Micro | System 600, uppercase, +8% | 11 / 14 | Caption 2 | 40 |

A role's size is its default size times (the mapped text style's system size at the current content size category / that style's default size). Line height and tracking scale with the size. Every role except Reading sits at its style's default size, so it equals the system style's size at every one of the twelve categories. **This replaces `UIFontMetrics`.** Measured on the iOS 26.5 simulator, `UIFontMetrics.scaledValue(for:compatibleWith:)` and `scaledFont(for:compatibleWith:)` do not reproduce the system sizes (Body at AX5: 48 pt against the system's 53; Footnote at AX5: 37.7 against 44; Caption 2 at XL: 15.3 against 13; largest deviation 6.3 pt), so roles scaled through them would not "track system roles exactly" as Section 4.4.3 intends.

**Reading.** The reader's size setting is a base size within 14-28 at the default text size (clamped), which then scales with Body like every other role (Section 4.4.3 gives sizes "at the default Dynamic Type setting"; principle 5 wants Dynamic Type "including the reader").

**Bold Text.** Every role's weight rises by 200, capped at 900 (400 to 600, 500 to 700, 600 to 800), from `legibilityWeight` (UIKit traits or the SwiftUI environment).

**macOS.** macOS has no Dynamic Type scale. Each role keeps its proportion to its mapped style at the macOS text-style size (`NSFont.preferredFont(forTextStyle:)`): Display 26, Title 22, Heading 17, Reading 14.5, Body 13, Callout 11, Caption 10, Micro 10 on macOS 26.6. Flagged for design review against the Mac companion board.

**SwiftUI.** `.sylviaFont(_:readingSize:monospaced:weight:)` reads `dynamicTypeSize` and `legibilityWeight` from the environment and applies font, tracking, line spacing (to reach the role's line height) and the Micro role's uppercase.

### 5. Marker set and capture kinds

| Marker | Category | Glyph | Label | Text / tint | Frame | Body |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| quote | capture kind | “ set in Literata (SF equivalent `quote.opening`) | QUOTE | Ochre / quote tint | none | Literata, Reading or Body |
| note | capture kind | `pencil.tip` | NOTE | Pine / none | none | system |
| audio | capture kind | `waveform` | AUDIO | audio / audio tint | none | never text (play control and waveform) |
| generated | capture kind | `sylvia.prompt` (custom) | GENERATED | generated / generated tint | 1 pt dashed | Body 600 question, Body answer |
| word | capture kind | `book` | WORD | Ochre / quote tint | none | Literata 600 headword |
| attention | status | `exclamationmark.triangle` | NEEDS ATTENTION | attention / attention tint | tinted row | Body reason |
| pending | status | `circle.fill` | QUEUED, WAITING | muted / pending tint | none | Caption |

Labels are stored in sentence case in the String Catalog and shown uppercase by the Micro role, so VoiceOver reads words, not letters. Glyphs and labels are unique per marker (tested), so no marker depends on hue.

**Capture kinds (Appendix A.1).** `text_quote` (from the plan) maps to quote; proposed for the capture-envelope schema: `note`, `audio_capture`, `generated_answer`, `word`. Matching is exact and case-sensitive. Any other kind, including the empty string and `TEXT_QUOTE`, resolves to the Pending marker with the raw kind as its label and never to Quote (tested, including 2,000 random strings). A kind that is empty or only white space shows the label "Unknown kind" (`marker.unknown_kind`, exported as `capture_kind_mapping.unrecognized.empty_label_key`), so the Pending marker always carries a visible and spoken label (review round 1; `ComponentLogicTests.emptyKindLabel` checks the row's text and VoiceOver text).

**Custom symbols.** `sylvia.prompt` (chevron and underscore) and `sylvia.capture` (brackets around three bars) take their geometry from the boards (`Thoughts.dc.html`, `Player.dc.html`; 24-unit view box, 1.75 stroke, round caps and joins). They ship as SF Symbols templates (template v3.0) in `Symbols.xcassets` with Ultralight, Regular and Black masters at the Small scale, from which the system interpolates every weight and scale. Strokes become filled capsules with an identical path structure in each master (required for interpolation; tested). Master stroke widths in template units: 2.6, 8.0 and 17.0 (prompt), 2.6, 8.0 and 10.5 (capture; its Black master is narrower so the three bars, 12 units apart, stay separate). Regular was calibrated against `chevron.right` and `waveform`. They load as symbol images, grow with the text style under Dynamic Type, gain ink at every weight step from Ultralight to Black, match `waveform`'s height (capture) and carry accessibility labels "Generated" and "Capture".

### 6. Status vocabulary

Every phrase of the Section 4.4.6 table (draft 0.3) is a `VocabularyKey` with a stable dotted key (the four keys in the A.5 sketch are kept), its moment and state machine, its placeholders and its plan source, compiled into `Vocabulary.xcstrings` (English, manual extraction). Additions only where the table calls for a form: `save.done_at` ("Saved {time}", the time after the durable commit, in Monospace), `import.bytes_verified` ("{verified} of {total} verified", as in Section 15.5) and the three endpoint rows with the model identifier. Product code renders a typed `Status` (for example `.waitingFor(source:)`, `.bytesVerified(verified:total:)`, `.indexingChapters(indexed:total:)`, `.endpoint(host:kind:model:)`, `.savedAt(_:)`) with `status.text()`; tests assert that the String Catalog output equals the template for every key.

- Plurals: "Indexing {n} of {m} chapter(s)" uses a String Catalog plural substitution.
- Bytes: decimal units chosen by the total, one decimal; the verified figure is rounded down and, while incomplete, always shown below the total (property-tested on 5,000 random pairs), so the row never claims more verified data than exists.
- Durations: "20h 58m", "42m", "59s" (whole units, truncated), "{duration} left".
- Times: the locale's short time style. The capture sheet's "Saved {time}" sets the time run in the Monospace face (SF Mono at the label's size and weight) and the rest in the button's face (`SaveStateLabel`; review round 1 found the whole label in SF Pro).
- Microcopy: sentence case, no exclamation marks (tested over every string).
- The forbidden list keeps the table's qualifiers ("Complete" is forbidden only before Ready; it is a permitted model state) and separates the three behaviors that no string check can catch.
- The import state mapping of Section 4.4.6 is `ImportState.presentation(indexingContinues:)`, including the pill glyph (dot, check, triangle), colors, and the reason-and-next-step requirement; `cancelled` leaves the Inbox.
- `lint.permitted_phrases` and `lint.forbidden_phrases` in the export are the machine-readable lists for lint and T54.

### 7. Shape, space, elevation, motion

As Section 4.4.4: grid 4; steps 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32; gutters 20 (iPhone) and 32 (iPad); radii 8 block, 10 control, 12 button, 14 card, 22 sheet, full pill; one shadow 0/8/24. ### 8. Lint

`Scripts/lint-design.py lint` scans product UI targets (`Apps/**` and any package under `Packages/` other than Design whose sources import SwiftUI, UIKit or AppKit; never `Spikes/`, `Packages/Design`, build products or test directories). Code rules run over the whole file with comments removed and string contents blanked, line breaks kept (review round 2: they used to run one line at a time, so a call wrapped by swift-format, such as `.font(.system(` with `size: 13))` on the next line, or `Color(` with `red: ...` on the next line, escaped every rule); a finding is reported on the line where the match starts. It rejects:

- **Colors:** hex color strings, `#colorLiteral`, hex color initializers, color component initializers of Color, UIColor, NSColor, CGColor and CIColor also when spelled `Color.init(...)` or as a bare `.init(red:|white:|hue:|...)`, any `.colorset`, and **named system colors** (decided in review round 1, because CLAUDE.md says product UI "never declares a literal color" and a named color is one): `Color.red`, `UIColor.label`, `NSColor.labelColor` and any other static member of the color types, any `.system<Color>` name (`.systemBlue`, `.systemBackground`), a bare palette or hierarchical name (`.red`, `.black`, `.secondary`, `.accentColor`, ...) as the argument of a color modifier (`foregroundStyle`, `background`, `fill`, `stroke`, `tint`, `shadow(color:)`, ...) or followed by `.opacity`, `.gradient`, `.mix` or `.shadow`, and a color looked up by a string name (`Color("Brand")`, `UIColor(named:)`). Review round 2 added: a palette name as a ternary branch among a color modifier's top-level arguments (`.foregroundStyle(on ? .red : .blue)`; labels inside nested calls such as `.overlay(Badge(role: .secondary))` are not branches) or of a value typed as a color; a named color typed as a color (`let c: Color = .red`, `: UIColor = .label`, `var c: Color { .red }`, `-> Color { return .blue }`); bridged (`Color(uiColor: .label)`, `Color(.secondaryLabel)`); assigned to a color property (`view.backgroundColor = .white`, `layer.borderColor = .black`) or passed to a color setter (`setTitleColor(.white, for:)`); and, as rule `color-provider`, a dynamic color built in a UI target (`UIColor { _ in .black }`, `UIColor(dynamicProvider:)`, `NSColor(name:dynamicProvider:)`), because appearance variants come only from the asset catalog. Allowed: `Color.clear` (it draws nothing; the layout-spacer idiom) and `.tint` (the environment tint, set from a token).
- **Font sizes:** `.system(size:)`, any `*Font(ofSize:)` factory (`systemFont`, `boldSystemFont`, `monospacedDigitSystemFont`, AppKit's `userFont`, ...), `UIFont`/`NSFont(name:size:)` and `(descriptor:size:)` (also `.init`), `UIFontDescriptor`/`NSFontDescriptor(name:size:)`, `.custom(_:size:)` and `.custom(_:fixedSize:)` (the size label anywhere in the balanced argument list), `.withSize(_:)`, a size in font descriptor attributes (`[.size: 22]`, `AttributeName.size`, `kCTFontSizeAttribute`; review round 2), and Core Text `CTFontCreate*` and `CTFontDescriptorCreate*Size*` calls.
- **Phrases:** any string literal (or String Catalog value) that is a permitted or forbidden phrase, also with leading or trailing punctuation or symbols ("Saved.", "Done!", "Downloading…", "Saved ✓") or wrapped in the Markdown that SwiftUI's `Text` renders from a literal (review round 2: `"**Saved**"`, `"_Done_"`, `"~~Ready~~"`, a code span, the text of a link `[Saved](url)`; emphasis markers are removed only at word boundaries, so "snake_case_identifier" is left alone); that has a punctuation-separated segment equal to one ("Error: the file could not be read", "Upload finished. Done!", "Middlemarch · Ready"; a separator is `: ; , . ! ? …` next to a space or the end, or `· • | — – ✓`, so identifiers such as "ask.generated" are not split); or that contains a multi-word phrase. Swift interpolation matches placeholders; matching ignores case.

A path given on the command line must exist, must not be excluded and must contain a Swift or strings file; otherwise the lint exits 2 instead of reporting "0 finding(s)" (review round 1: an explicit path under `.build` or a `*Tests` directory used to be skipped silently). Comments are ignored. `self-test` runs it over `Packages/Design/LintFixtures` (violating samples annotated line by line with the expected rule, including `Bypasses.swift` with every spelling the round 1 review found unflagged and `Multiline.swift` with every spelling the round 2 review found (calls wrapped over lines, ternary branches, typed, bridged and assigned named colors, color providers, Markdown phrases, descriptor size attributes); a clean sample of near-misses such as `.fontWeight(.black)`, `.buttonStyle(.sylvia(compact ? .primary : .secondary))`, `.padding(compact ? .horizontal : .vertical)`, `.overlay(Badge(role: .secondary))`, token colors chosen by a ternary over several lines, `extension UIColor {}`, `-> UIColor { UIColor(ColorToken...) }`, `Color.clear`, `"**Middlemarch**, chapter 20"` and "Error handling is in chapter 3"; a synthetic tree that checks target discovery; and the explicit-path refusals).

**What the lint does not catch** (left to review and to the snapshot and vocabulary tests; each was confirmed against the lint with a probe file in review round 2): colors and sizes computed at run time from non-literal values passed to APIs it does not name; a palette name passed to a parameter the rules do not name (`MyView(highlight: .red)`; the labels `color:`, `tint:`, `foreground:`, `background:`, `fill:` and `stroke:` are named) or returned from a body with more than one statement (`switch k { case 0: return .red ... }` in a function returning `Color`); a color type reached through a type alias (`typealias C = Color; C(red: ...)`); system text-style fonts such as `.font(.body)` or `.font(.system(.body))`, which are not literal sizes but bypass the type roles (a review rule; Section 4.4.9); scaling text with `.minimumScaleFactor` or `.scaleEffect`; single-word phrases next to an interpolation without punctuation (`"\(n) Done"`); and status text assembled from fragments that are not phrases on their own (`"Sav" + "ed"`). Words that are phrases only in context ("Delete", "Complete") are flagged wherever they stand alone in a literal; product code takes them from the vocabulary.

### 9. Grayscale and Increase Contrast conformance method (T53)

1. **Tokens (automated, part 1).** Every marker has a unique glyph and a unique label; Note has no tint; Generated alone has the dashed frame. The export check prints each tint's grey level after a luminance grayscale conversion (`contrast-export.md`): the Paper tints collapse to 225-233, which is why glyph, label and body form must carry the distinction (Section 4.4.8). Increase Contrast is verified from the compiled catalog: hairline 24%, tints one step stronger, the three text overrides.
2. **Components (automated, part 2; simulator iPhone 17, iOS 26.5).**
   - *Rendering.* Each sample in `Sources/SylviaDesignGallery/Gallery.swift` (moved from the test target in part 3) is hosted in a `UIHostingController` in a window 402 pt wide (iPhone 17; the sidebar sample 320 pt, regular width) with trait overrides for appearance, `accessibilityContrast`, content size (Large or AX5), `legibilityWeight` and display scale (2x at Large, 1x at AX5 to keep the references small), and rendered with `layer.render(in:)` into an opaque 8-bit sRGB bitmap.
   - *Grayscale conversion.* Each pixel becomes the WCAG relative luminance of its sRGB value (0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B on linearized channels), re-encoded with the sRGB transfer function as one 8-bit grey level. This keeps every WCAG contrast ratio and removes hue. Grey references are one-channel PNGs compared byte for byte; color references use swift-snapshot-testing's exact comparison (precision 1, perceptual precision 1).
   - *Matrix.* Every sample in light, dark, light + Increase Contrast and dark + Increase Contrast, at Large and AX5, each with its grayscale rendering, plus Bold Text (light, Large): 22 samples x 17 = 374 references, about 26 MB, in part 2; 31 samples and 527 references (about 37 MB) with the part 3 screen parts. References are recorded only on `Sylvia-M0-g06-design` (iPhone 17, iOS 26.5); the suite refuses to compare on another device or runtime. CI runs with `SNAPSHOT_TESTING_RECORD=never`, so a missing reference fails, and a negative check replaces one reference with another image and requires the suite to fail on exactly that sample.
   - *Pairs that differ in meaning.* Markers (21 pairs): glyph identifiers differ, glyph shapes differ in grayscale (ink cropped and resampled to a 24 x 24 mask; at least 10% of cells differ), and the spoken labels from the accessibility tree differ. Status pills (36 pairs over the nine import-state pills): every pair reads differently; pairs with different glyphs also differ in glyph shape (same 10% rule); pairs with the same glyph share fill and text color, so their only and intended difference is the phrase. Availability badges: the top edge of "On this device" is one solid run and the others five or more dashed runs, and their labels differ. Cards: the Generated frame is ten or more runs, the Quote and Note frames one. Quote and Audio cards differ by form: a Literata quote block versus a play button and waveform, and no Reading-role text in the audio card.
   - *Measured levels.* On the grayscale `buttons` sample in all four appearances: primary fill against the ground at least 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11); its label against the fill at least 4.5:1; primary fill against ink text at least **1.5:1** (proposed threshold so a filled button never reads as a block of text; measured 2.31 light, 2.06 dark); the secondary interior equal to the ground and its hairline above 1.1:1 against it; the destructive border at least 3:1; the secondary hairline stronger under Increase Contrast (measured 1.27 to 1.66 light, 1.42 to 1.97 dark). Rating: every full star covers at least 1.5x the area of the most-covered empty star at 1.5:1 contrast against the ground, and the half star lies between.
   - *Controls.* The measurement code is checked against known cases: the same glyph twice gives identical masks; the solid frames above give one run.
3. **Layout (T51 proxy, automated).** Every text set in a type role reports its laid-out frame and the height it needs at that width with no line limit (`LayoutAudit`, installed only by tests and the catalog). At Large, AX5 and AX5 with Bold Text, no sample may have a truncated text, a text outside the canvas, or an accessibility element outside the canvas. A control renders a deliberately truncated and a deliberately pushed-out label and requires the audit to flag both. Not caught: words broken in the middle (wrapping, not truncation), overlaps between siblings, and anything drawn without a type role (covers).
4. **Simulator checks in the real app (review round 1).** `AreaNavigationTests` in the catalog's UI tests: Command-1/2/3 from simulated keyboard events select Library, Thoughts and Inbox on the iPhone 17 (tab bar) and iPad (A16) (sidebar) simulators; a long press on the Inbox tab bar item at AX5 shows the large content viewer, captured from the host with `simctl` while the test holds the press and compared with two unpressed captures. Review round 2: lifting the finger must open the Inbox, a press on Thoughts slid to Library must open the Library and only it, and long presses of 0.35 s and 3 s on the player's Play/Pause, at AX5 and at the default size, must each toggle it exactly once (a run with the lift activation removed fails).
5. **Devices and people.** The component set with Color Filters set to Grayscale, with Increase Contrast, and at AX5 on the oldest supported iPhone, the large content viewer on every item, VoiceOver and a physical hardware keyboard are steps 12-21 of `Docs/Stage0/device-checklists/G06-Design.md`.

### 11. Design catalog and interaction prototype (G0.6 part 3)

**The app.** `Apps/DesignCatalog` (XcodeGen, bundle identifier `com.jevidon.sylvia.designcatalog`, iPhone and iPad, iOS 26.0, Swift 6 language mode with complete strict concurrency) depends only on the local package (`SylviaDesign` and `SylviaDesignGallery`). ## Consequences

- Screens get colors, type, markers and status text only from this package; the lint enforces it from the first UI target on.
- Android or another client implements the same system from `marginalia.v1.json` and its schema, including the derivation rules, overrides, capture-kind mapping, vocabulary and custom-symbol geometry.
- Token changes go through `sylvia-design-export write`, which regenerates the catalog, String Catalog, export and fixtures together; CI fails on drift.
- Type scaling depends on the system's text-style sizes, not `UIFontMetrics`; if Apple changes the style sizes, roles follow them.
- Components are the only way screens get bars, buttons, pills, cards and sheets; a screen that needs a variant asks for it here. Snapshot references must be re-recorded (on the reference simulator only) with every token, role or component change, and reviewed before they are committed.
- The snapshot references are tied to iOS 26.5 rendering; a runtime or Xcode change requires a re-baseline and a review of the diff.
- To re-check: device appearance of all of the above, the Mac's real Increase Contrast setting (tests resolve it through SwiftUI because `NSAppearance(named: .accessibilityHighContrastAqua)` returns plain Aqua), and the custom symbols' visual weight against SF Symbols by a designer.

## Alternatives considered

- **Scale Literata with `UIFontMetrics` (as Section 4.4.3 says).** Rejected on evidence: it does not reproduce the system sizes on iOS 26.5 (up to 6.3 pt off).
- **Use the printed dark tints.** Not chosen: the plan says the derived tints are to be set from the rule; the printed values are kept in the export (`plan_listed_dark`) for design review.
- **Literal "darken" in Night under Increase Contrast.** Rejected: it lowers tint-to-ground separation.
- **Lower the 4.5:1 threshold or exempt Increase Contrast.** Not allowed (Section 4.4.2; brief).
- **Ship the custom glyphs as PDF or SVG images.** Rejected: images do not follow symbol weight or text-style scale like SF Symbols.
- **Hand-maintained JSON or asset catalog.** Rejected by Section 4.4.9; one generator keeps them consistent.
- **System `TabView` and `NavigationStack` bars.** Rejected by Section 4.4.7 (draft 0.3): they take the Liquid Glass treatment on the iOS 27 SDK. Sylvia's bars replicate the system controls' traits, selection, large content viewer and keyboard shortcuts instead.
- **Snapshot-testing's own image strategies for SwiftUI views.** Not used: the tests also need the layout audit and the accessibility tree from the same render, and a grayscale comparison without color management.
- **Grayscale by `CIColorControls` saturation 0 or a device-like filter.** Rejected: saturation removal does not preserve luminance, so measured ratios would not equal WCAG ratios. The iOS Color Filters grayscale is checked on a device instead (checklist step 13).
- **SwiftLint for the literal checks.** Not installed (ADR-0002); the purpose-built script checks exactly the Section 4.4.9 rules and has its own self-test.
