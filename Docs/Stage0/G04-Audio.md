# G0.4 audio: playback, excerpts and quick capture

**Gate:** G0.4 (plan 21.1): "test the proposed codec/container matrix, long playback, chapters, cross-file excerpts, export timing and main-position-preserving preview. Separately evaluate lock-screen quick capture. Record which part of the aspirational workflow can ship."
**Spike:** `Spikes/G04-Audio` (XcodeGen project `G04Audio`: framework `G04AudioKit`, app `G04AudioHarness`, control extension `G04CaptureControl`, test bundles `G04AudioTests` and `G04QuickCaptureUITests`). Stage 0 evidence code; never shipped.
**Decisions:** ADR-0006 (playback, timeline and excerpts; provisional codec list), ADR-0007 (quick capture). Both Proposed.
**Device steps:** `Docs/Stage0/device-checklists/G04-Audio.md` (all pending).

## Decisions for the product owner

Nothing here is decided: the spike's choices are proposals (review R6-04: they had been stated only inside long rows). Each decision names its evidence; approving, changing or rejecting one changes ADR-0006 or ADR-0007 and the tests that pin it. Agent-proposed; product-owner approval pending.

## How to reproduce

```sh
Scripts/fixtures/fetch.sh                                   # LibriVox sources (SHA-256 pinned)
Scripts/ci.sh g04                                           # Scripts/ci.d/60-g04-audio.sh: everything below; tables in .build/ci-results/g04-audio
G04_RESULTS_DIR=Docs/Stage0/evidence/G04-Audio/results Scripts/ci.sh g04   # the same, writing the evidence tables there
G04_RESULTS_DIR=Docs/Stage0/evidence/G04-Audio/results G04_DEVICE_FIXTURES=1 Scripts/ci.sh g04   # as the clean run: also the device bundle with the fixtures
```

`G04_RESULTS_DIR` may be relative (taken from the repository root) or absolute; the drop-in empties that directory, passes the absolute path to the test process, and fails if any of the 41 expected tables (42 with `G04_DEVICE_FIXTURES=1`; `EXPECTED_TABLES` in the drop-in) is missing or older than the run. A table the tests cannot write is a test failure. The drop-in generates the fixtures if needed (`Scripts/fixtures/generate-g04-audio-fixtures.sh`, `generate-g04-audio-long-formats.sh`, `generate-g04-audio-probes.sh` and `generate-g04-audio-stereo.sh`, Homebrew ffmpeg 8.1.2, about 30 minutes and 8.5 GB the first time), generates the project, builds the tests for a generic iOS device with automatic signing and records the signatures (unsigned when the host has no Apple Development identity), with `G04_DEVICE_FIXTURES=1` also builds and signs that bundle with the 8.7 GB of fixtures inside and checks every bundled file against the fixture manifests (row 30), builds for and runs every test on its own simulator (`G04_SIM_NAME`, default `Sylvia-M0-g04-audio`; round 6's clean runs used `Sylvia-M0-minors-g04-audio`), samples the host's load every 30 s while it runs (`host-load.tsv`; review R4-02), prints the App Intents metadata of the app and the control extension, kills and relaunches the harness app (process-kill trial), decodes kept excerpts with ffmpeg and measures those decodes (portability), runs the quick-capture intent through the system, as the App Shortcut from the Shortcuts app and as the control from Control Center (UI tests), with the build as Xcode signs it and re-signed by the team, and fails unless the quick-capture results this report relies on are there (row 18; review R5-06), the presses with nothing heard among them, and unless every UI test's capture count agrees with the harness log (reviews R6-01, R6-06), shuts down its simulator when it exits, whether it passed or failed (unless `G04_KEEP_SIM=1`; review R4-04), and exits non-zero on any failure or build warning, on a test file that creates a player outside `TestAudio`, or on a harness launch that did not log the quiet volume. Everything plays at 1 % volume unless `SYLVIA_AUDIBLE_PLAYBACK=1` (row 34). A missing fixture is a test failure, never a skip. The final clean run took 91 minutes (the drop-in's header estimates about 90), 40 of them the soak test, 17 the long-format suite and 3 the stereo suite.

## Findings that change scope or approach

1. **Start/resume: a constant-bit-rate MP3 meets the budget at every depth with the per-file timing mode; the files that must stay on precise timing miss it wherever opening the file or the first seek in it grows with length** (rows 21, 22, 33 and 35). Estimated timing is exact only in a constant-bit-rate MP3 and seconds to minutes off in VBR MP3, native FLAC (with or without a seek table) and Ogg Opus (row 22), so the import decides per file (ADR-0006 decision 2): it proves an MP3 constant by walking every frame header, and the player, the preview and the export then open it with estimated timing: sample-identical decodes, the same content positions as precise timing, and a cold resume in 0.14-0.15 s at every depth to 10 h, at host loads of 6.8-32.5 in the final clean run (11-74 in round 4's). LibriVox audiobooks, where the plan's fixtures come from, are 64 kb/s CBR MP3 with LAME `Info` tags; both LibriVox section files and the four book members here are proved. With precise timing, on the simulator: (a) the first seek in an MP3 the process has not read costs about 1 s per hour of depth, more when the host is loaded (the budget crossing lay between about 1.0 and 1.8 h in the runs of rounds 3 to 6, and at 0.41-1.52 h in the round-4 review's run at load averages of 60-148), and (b) opening a native FLAC, an MP3 without a header or an Ogg Opus file scans it, about 2.7-3.4, 0.8-1.0 and 0.09-0.16 s per hour of file over six runs, at every resume depth, and longer under host load (a 10-minute native FLAC took 2.65 s to open during a load spike in the round-5 review's run, 0.45-0.46 s in the final clean run): a 10-hour native FLAC took 28.1-30.4 s to resume in the final clean run, a 10-hour MP3 without a header 9.0-9.5 s and a 10-hour Ogg Opus 1.0-1.6 s. Relaunching the app always takes this path (the service opens the member and seeks to the stored checkpoint), and so do a capture's export and pending preview in such a file (29.1 s from the 10-hour FLAC, 14.3-15.5 s 9.5 h into a 10-hour MP3 with precise timing, against 0.07 s for the proved-CBR MP3 with the per-file mode; the anchor and note are saved first). 3. **Chapters depend on the container.** QuickTime chapter tracks (M4A/M4B with AAC-LC, HE-AAC, ALAC, FLAC or Opus) and ID3 CHAP frames are read exactly; Vorbis-comment chapters in FLAC and Ogg are read with an indefinite last end (the importer closes it at the duration) and only in the three-digit `CHAPTERxxx` form (`CHAPTER01` is ignored; `container-probes.txt`); WAV cue points are not read at all; CAF chapters were not tested (row 4).
4. **The excerpt's stored "actual" timing is the decoder's claim, not the truth.** For MP3 sources it is 13.1 ms off, for HE-AAC 47.9 ms (constant per codec). Within budget; each range's source format is now stored with the excerpt so a correction can be added if a later budget needs it.
5. **AVQueuePlayer posts end-of-item before advancing.** A position store that reads the player's current item at that moment saves the old member. Fixed in the spike (ADR-0006 decision 10); the product must keep it.
6. **Furthest position needs an explicit rule for forward jumps.** The first rule ("contiguous playback from the frontier") froze the frontier for the rest of the book after any skip forward (review R1-02). The rule now adopts a run that started ahead of the frontier after 30 s of contiguous listening (ADR-0006 decision 9). As a result the furthest position is a place reached by listening, not proof that everything before it was heard; completion stays explicit (plan 12).
7. **Lock-screen quick capture is plausible only through an App Intent, and it needs a device to decide.** On the simulator, unlocked, an App Shortcut run from the Shortcuts app (team-signed build) and the control pressed in Control Center (both signings) were performed in the playing app's process and saved before acknowledging (row 18): the system took the `AudioPlaybackIntent` route the design relies on for the control. The bookmark remote command is reportedly not shown since iOS 16, and Live Activity buttons are documented as inactive while locked. A Control or App Shortcut can run while locked, but whether the system still performs the control's intent in the app process while locked, and whether adopting `AudioPlaybackIntent` for an action that does not start audio is acceptable, are open (ADR-0007). A quick capture must also know what "the moment" is when the app was not running: the system launches the app to perform the intent, the app restores the last book paused, and the intent can arrive before, during or after that restore (on the simulator it arrived during it). Under the first rule ("nothing loaded") the answer depended on that race; now a quick-capture surface captures only a moment heard in the app process, so a cold launch answers nothing to capture, deterministically (review R6-01; rows 17-18; device checklist step 12). Proposed narrowing for approval (plan 3.3): PO-6.
8. **Locked capture constrains storage protection.** Every store on the capture path (capture records, checkpoints, retained excerpts and their directories) is class C (`completeUntilFirstUserAuthentication`), set explicitly (review R5-04: the excerpt had been left to the platform default; `ExcerptProtectionTests`; the simulator ignores the class, `simulator-file-protection.txt`, so only a device reads it back, checklist step 9), as ADR-0012 sets for every persistent file: a stricter class makes the save fail while locked, and a weaker one is excluded (plan 18.1, T50). The trade-off, readable while locked once the phone has been unlocked since the restart, is in ADR-0007 decision 4. Before the first unlock after a restart nothing can be saved and nothing may be acknowledged. To be confirmed with G0.5's persistence decision.
9. **The excerpt export raised stereo speech by 3 dB; it now averages the two channels.** The first stereo fixtures (review R6-05) showed that asking AVAssetReader for one channel from a stereo file gives an equal-power sum, (L + R) x 0.707: a book with the same speech in both channels, the usual stereo audiobook, came out 3 dB louder in its excerpts (gain 1.414), above full scale where the source is mastered within 3 dB of it. The decoder now averages the two channels (gain 0.998); mono files and HE-AAC v2, which the system reports as one channel, are unchanged. HE-AAC v2 does not keep two different channels apart (parametric stereo), which matters only for books whose channels carry different content. Row 37; ADR-0006 decision 13; PO-4.
