# G0.6 Design tokens on devices: parts 1-3 (package, components, design catalog and interaction prototype)

**Scope.** Gate G0.6 (plan Section 21.1) in three parts. Part 1: the `SylviaDesign` package core in `Packages/Design` (color tokens, type roles, marker set, status vocabulary, the portable token export with its schema, contrast checks, design lint). Part 2: the Section 4.4.7 component library, its snapshot tests, and automated proxies for T51 and T53. Part 3: the design catalog app `Apps/DesignCatalog` (the component gallery for the device review and the M0 interaction prototype of Section 21.2), its UI smoke tests, and the screen parts the prototype needed. Decisions and final values: [ADR-0013](../ADRs/0013-design-language.md) (Proposed): Section 9 for the grayscale method, Section 10 for the components, Section 11 for the catalog and prototype.

**Environments.**

- **Host:** macOS 26.6.2 (25G83), Apple M5 Max, Xcode 27.0 (27A266a), Swift 6.4.
- **Simulator:** `Sylvia-M0-g06-design`, iPhone 17 (iPhone18,3), iOS 26.5 (23F77). All snapshot references were recorded and compared here, and the catalog's iPhone UI tests run here.
- **Simulator (part 3):** `Sylvia-M0-g06-design-SE`, iPhone SE (3rd generation) (iPhone14,6), 375 x 667 pt, iOS 26.5, the narrowest and shortest iPhone screen iOS 26 supports; `Sylvia-M0-g06-design-iPad`, iPad (A16), iOS 26.5, for the regular-width layout.
- **Simulators (review round 3, on the integration branch):** `Sylvia-M0-minors-g06-design` (iPhone 17, iOS 26.5) with `-SE` and `-iPad` of the same device types and runtime; the snapshot references compare on any iPhone 17 with iOS 26.5 (`RecordingDevice`), whatever its name. The round 3 clean run and the R3-02 runs used these.
- **Device:** none used. Every device and human check is pending in [the checklist](device-checklists/G06-Design.md) (steps 1-31).

## Findings that change the plan's scope or approach

Part 1 (unchanged):

1. **`UIFontMetrics` does not make roles track system sizes.** Measured on iOS 26.5, `scaledValue` and `scaledFont` diverge from the system text-style sizes by up to 6.3 pt (Body at AX5: 48 against 53). The package scales each role by the mapped style's system size instead. Proposed plan change: "scale in proportion to the mapped text style's system size".
2. **Increase Contrast needs text overrides, and "darken" cannot apply literally in Night** (ADR-0013 Sections 2 and 3).
3. **The plan's printed dark tints do not follow its own 14% rule** (for example quote #31291A against the printed #3A2E17).
4. **Muted text fails on tints in Paper** (4.29-4.64:1). The components keep muted captions off tints ("No transcript" sits on the card surface).
5. **The cover title is Paper in both appearances**; the Night ground on the cover fields would give 2.10-3.16:1.

Part 2:

6. **Snapshot references alone do not catch clipping.** The references an earlier agent recorded had a truncated destructive button label ("Delete recor…") and a truncated mini-player caption ("20h 58m l…") at AX5, and they passed the snapshot suite because the references contained the defects. The T51 layout audit found the button and the overflowing rating row; review of the images found the rest (mini-player, timecodes broken across lines, cover titles broken mid-word). All are fixed and the audit now runs in CI. Approach: snapshot tests need the layout audit beside them, and new references need human review before they are committed.
7. **The large content viewer cannot be verified through the accessibility elements.** SwiftUI does not surface `.accessibilityShowsLargeContentViewer` through them. The catalog's UI tests observe it from the host instead (screenshots during the press, and the item that opens on lift; review rounds 1 and 2); every item and legibility stay a device check (step 12).
8. **System sheets do not follow the plan's motion values.** Section 4.4.4 asks for 200 ms ease-out sheets and no motion other than opacity under Reduce Motion. Sylvia presents sheets with the system `.sheet`, whose timing and Reduce Motion behavior are the system's. Proposed: accept the system sheet motion (and amend 4.4.4), or budget a custom presentation. Device step 19 records what the system does.
9. **Bars stop growing at xxxLarge.** The tab bar, navigation bar and reader chrome cap their text at xxxLarge and rely on the large content viewer, as the system bars they replace do. Section 4.4.3 says "Dynamic Type everywhere". Proposed: state the bar exception in 4.4.7.
10. **"Hairline secondary buttons remain distinguishable" holds by fill, not by the hairline.** In grayscale the primary fill against the ground is 6.66:1 (light), but the secondary button's own hairline is only 1.27:1 against the ground (1.66:1 with Increase Contrast). The secondary button is recognized by its label and an outline that is faint by design. A designer should confirm this on a device (step 13).

Part 3:

11. **`isHittable` is not proof that a control is usable.** XCUITest calls an element hittable when any part of it is visible. The inherited reachability check passed with the Inbox's Resolve button mostly under the mini-player and with the failed-save button cut off at the bottom of the sheet (iPhone 17, AX5; screenshots from the inherited run, not kept). The check now requires the whole frame between the status bar and the mini-player (or the window bottom), steers short slow drags in both directions, and ignores the mini-player's own copy of a label. Approach for M2-M8 UI tests: assert frames against the obstructing chrome, not `isHittable`.
12. **The mini-player inset must be set per screen, not on the navigation stack.** With the mini-player as a `safeAreaInset` around each area's `NavigationStack`, the scroll views inside did not get it as a content inset: at AX5 on the iPhone SE the end of the Thoughts list stopped under the mini-player (bottom edge of the last quotation at y 557, mini-player top at 225.5) and could not be scrolled into view. Attached to each screen inside the stack, the end scrolls clear (177.5). [`miniplayer-inset-diagnosis-simulator.md`](evidence/G06-Design/miniplayer-inset-diagnosis-simulator.md). Applies to every product screen in M2-M4 that shows the mini-player.
13. **Sheets open at full height.** `sylviaSheet` sets no detents, so the system presents the capture sheet and the impact report at full height, and short content leaves empty surface below it (`screens/iphone/light/capture.saved.png`, `storage.impact.png`), where the boards show a sheet sized to its content over the dimmed screen. Proposed: content-sized detents in M3, with the full-height detent kept for AX sizes where the content is taller than the screen. Design decision (step 31).
14. **Work titles need fitting at AX5.** In the Title role at AX5, "Middlemarch" (58 pt) is wider than a 362 pt column and SwiftUI broke it inside the word. `FittingText` (package) steps the Dynamic Type size down one step at a time, never below Large, until the longest word fits; used for area, sheet and work titles only. Section 4.4.3 says every role scales with the user's text size; this is a bounded exception that the plan should state (ADR-0013 Section 11).
15. **The mini-player is large at accessibility sizes.** It grows with Dynamic Type: at AX5 it is 359.5 pt tall on the iPhone SE (54% of a 667 pt screen; measured frame) and about a third of the iPhone 17 screen (estimated from the screenshots). Lists still scroll clear of it (Finding 12), but only a narrow band of content is visible. Proposed: treat the mini-player like the bars (stop growing at xxxLarge and rely on the large content viewer), or collapse it to one row at accessibility sizes. Product and design decision; device step 26 records how it feels on hardware.

Review round 1:

16. **Token pairs alone do not cover what components draw.** Measuring component states found two failures the 44 token pairs could not see: "Saving…" on the disabled primary button at 2.07:1 (light) and 2.56:1 (dark), and every button label while pressed at 0.7 opacity (3.23:1 destructive, 3.38:1 primary, Paper). "Saving…" now keeps full contrast while the button is busy, and the pressed opacity is 0.9, the smallest step that keeps 4.5:1 (ADR-0013 Section 3, for design review). Disabled buttons remain exempt as inactive controls, with the rule that no status phrase is ever shown on one. Approach for M2-M8: every new component state that changes color or opacity adds a component pair.
17. **SwiftUI's large content viewer does not open the item on lift.** In the simulator a long press on the Inbox tab item at AX5 showed the viewer, but lifting the finger left the Library open, where a system tab bar opens the item under the finger: the viewer cancels the Button's touch and SwiftUI does not forward the activation. Fixed in review round 2 with an explicit activation (a UIKit long-press recognizer that runs alongside the Button and reports where the finger lifted, plus a gate so the action never runs twice; ADR-0013 Section 10); the simulator tests now require it. Approach for M2-M8: any custom control with `.accessibilityShowsLargeContentViewer` must also get the lift activation (`sylviaLiftActivation`, or `sylviaBarLiftActivation` for a bar).

Review round 3:

18. **The large content viewer takes a press over sooner than the lift activation assumed.** The round 2 lift activation began its recognizer after 0.25 s, assuming the viewer's own delay is longer. A timing probe in a scratch build on the iPhone 17 simulator at AX5 found that the viewer let presses of up to 0.149 s through as taps and took a 0.161 s press over; with the 0.25 s start such a press activated nothing (no Button action, no lift activation). The round 2 UI test pressed only for 0.35 and 3 s, so it passed. The recognizer now begins after 0.05 s, and the test also presses for 0.15 and 0.2 s ([`large-content-viewer-timing-probe-simulator.md`](evidence/G06-Design/large-content-viewer-timing-probe-simulator.md), [`large-content-viewer-mutations-simulator.md`](evidence/G06-Design/large-content-viewer-mutations-simulator.md)). The delay on a device is not measured; checklist step 12 adds a press just long enough to show the viewer. Approach for M2-M8: test a custom control's touch handling at durations around the system's own thresholds, not only well inside them.

## Review round 3 (after M0 integration)

The round 3 review (verdict accept-with-minor; 2 minor findings, 3 gate gaps) is addressed on branch `m0-int-minors-g06-design`, from the integration commit b354dc7. Every change is built and tested in the round 3 clean run (header).

- **R3-01, the lint missed palette names in gradient color lists, `listItemTint` and shape-style values:** fixed, with rules for those spellings and for the further spellings of the same classes found while fixing them (the `ListItemTint` factories, SwiftUI's semantic system styles, `??` defaults, `mix(with:)`, computed and conditional color lists, dictionaries with color values, Canvas shading); 41 `lint-expect` samples in `Styles.swift` and near-misses in `clean/`. The round 2 lint reported none of the reviewer's four probe lines, the current one all four, and the new rules add no finding over the 343 Swift files of the package and the spikes ([`lint-r3-probe-host.log`](evidence/G06-Design/lint-r3-probe-host.log), written by [`commands/r3_lint_probe.py`](evidence/G06-Design/commands/r3_lint_probe.py); row "Lint"). ADR-0013 Section 8 names the new rules and the gaps that remain (a list passed to a parameter the rules do not name, other collections, materials and token gradients), each confirmed with a probe.
- **R3-02, the lift activation relied on a fixed 150 ms settle delay:** fixed in the gate rather than documented. Either order now activates once, and a Button action is taken for the latest press whose touch lifted (row "Tab bar and bar items: large content viewer"; ADR-0013 Section 10). Unit tests prove both orders on host and simulator, also when a new touch begins before the late action arrives; a timing probe in a scratch build shows the order the gate relies on (the Button's action 0 to 1 ms after the lift, [`large-content-viewer-timing-probe-simulator.md`](evidence/G06-Design/large-content-viewer-timing-probe-simulator.md), with its commands in `evidence/G06-Design/commands/` and the raw probe lines in `evidence/G06-Design/large-content-viewer-timing-probe/`); mutation checks show the tests depend on it ([`large-content-viewer-mutations-simulator.md`](evidence/G06-Design/large-content-viewer-mutations-simulator.md)). The two stated limits (a touch-free activation close to a lift activation counts as that press's activation; a whole second tap during a stall can let a press activate twice) and the feel on hardware are checklist step 12: pending-device, and pending accessibility review.
- **Found while fixing R3-02 (Finding 18):** at accessibility sizes, presses between about 0.15 and 0.25 s activated nothing, because the viewer takes a press over sooner than the 0.25 s at which the lift recognizer began. Fixed (the recognizer begins at 0.05 s), with test presses of 0.15 and 0.2 s.
- **Gate gaps, unchanged and still pending:** the G0.6 device checks (Color Filters grayscale, Increase Contrast, Bold Text and the largest text on the oldest supported iPhone, the iPhone 17 Pro and an iPad, and step 12's large content viewer feel) need hardware; the T54 walk over every state machine's strings waits for the state machines (M1); custom-symbol weight matching and the design review of the ADR-0013 token adjustments need a designer. Agent-reviewed; human sign-off pending.
- **First round 3 attempt (unreviewed WIP on this branch, reviewed and replaced):** it took a Button action for the latest press that had *begun*, so a touch beginning while a late action was on its way let that action run (mutation 1 reproduces it), and its clean run stopped halfway; the partial evidence it committed (the iPad and iPhone SE light reports and screenshots were missing) was replaced by the round 3 clean run.
